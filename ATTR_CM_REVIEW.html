Pooled result

Estimate withdrawn. BOTH TRIALS REGISTER HIERARCHICAL ENDPOINTS ANALYSED BY WIN RATIO, AND A WIN RATIO CANNOT BE RECOVERED FROM A 2x2 TABLE. ATTR-ACT's registered primary, verbatim, is a 'Hierarchical Combination of All-Cause Mortality and Frequency of Cardiovascular-Related Hospitalizations'. In such an analysis every patient in one arm is compared with every patient in the other and the result is a ratio of wins to losses; there is no numerator over a denominator to extract, and reducing it to counts of participants discards the hierarchy that defines it. AND THE TWO HIERARCHIES ARE NOT THE SAME HIERARCHY. ATTRibute-CM's registered primary is a 'Hierarchical Combination of All-Cause Mortality, Cumulative Frequency of CV-related Hospitalization, CHANGE FROM BASELINE IN NT-proBNP and CHANGE FROM BASELINE IN 6MWT'. That is FOUR levels to ATTR-ACT's two, and its lower two levels are CONTINUOUS CHANGE SCORES rather than events. Two hierarchies of different depth, whose tie-breaking rules differ and whose lower levels are not even the same kind of quantity, do not estimate one thing.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH: NOT that tafamidis or acoramidis is ineffective in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy. The trials here measure hierarchical win-ratio composites of death, hospitalisation and functional change, and nothing on this page estimates the question its title implies. WHAT IS CONFIRMED: the original withdrawal was RIGHT and its one-line reason -- 'win-ratio composite reduced to 2x2 counts' -- is exactly the defect, now sourced from both registrations. Both identities are correct. RE-VERIFICATION ADDED the second, independent ground the original did not name: the two hierarchies differ in depth and in kind. THE REPLACEMENT QUESTION IS PARKED: a synthesis of win ratios is possible in principle but requires each trial's own win-ratio estimate and its variance, which the registry's protocol record does not carry.
