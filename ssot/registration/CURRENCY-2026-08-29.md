# Currency as a query, not an essay

**`scripts/currency_query.py` — keyed to a date the comparator itself supplies, so it runs
on any topic where a published comparator is recorded.**

A published synthesis decays from the day its search closes and cannot report anything
later without redoing itself. Saying that is rhetoric. Returning the records is a finding.

⭐ **The ClinicalTrials.gov column is the strongest signal and the one to lead with.** It
counts trials that **posted results after the comparator was published** — registered
evidence that a review of that date *cannot* contain, identified by registration id rather
than by keyword. `rosuvastatin` has 42 such trials; `cangrelor`'s comparator is from 2013.

## ⚠️ What the literature counts are NOT

PubMed and Europe PMC return large numbers — 8,968 for rosuvastatin, 1,632 for icosapent.
**These are candidate records matching a term, not new eligible trials, and not one has been
screened.**

> Reporting "1,632 new records" as new evidence would be the same error as reporting
> "19 read" when 19 were retrieved and one was appraised.

The claim these support is narrow and true: *a synthesis published on date X cannot contain
a record that appeared after X.* Eligibility is a separate question and this does not touch
it.

## The date is deliberately conservative

The anchor is the comparator's **publication** date, not its **search** date. A review's
search closes before it publishes, often by 6–18 months, so records in that interval are
*also* new to it and are **not** counted here.

**Every number above is a lower bound.** It can understate our advantage; it cannot
overstate it, which is the direction an argument of ours should err in.

**A currency gap is not a defect in the comparator.** A 2021 review searching to 2020 was
right in 2021. The claim is only that it is not current now, and that the difference is
checkable.

## Two defects found and fixed while building this

**1. I overloaded Europe PMC and nearly reported it as absence.** The first pass took
**502/503/504 on 12 of 22 topics**, and five more came back as `hitCount 0`. Those zeros
were artefacts of my own request rate. With pacing and retries: **19 EXECUTED, 3 FAILED**,
and the "zeros" became real counts in the hundreds. Recording a server-side refusal as
"no new records" is the FAILED-versus-EMPTY confusion this project enforces everywhere
else, and it nearly went out in a report.

**2. The exception handler labelled a source with the name of my own function.** One record
carried `source: "since_europepmc"`. That is a fact about my code appearing in a field
reserved for facts about the world.

## The flagship example does not run, and that is the finding

⚠️ **`agyw-hiv-prep-review` — the dapivirine topic — returns `NO_COMPARATOR`.** The Cochrane
dapivirine review exists; it is **not recorded on the topic**, so there is no date to be
current against and the query cannot run.

The currency argument is strongest exactly where we cannot currently make it. **The fix is
one field**: record the comparator's PMID on the topic. I have not written one in, because
I could not identify the review with certainty — a 2023-08 candidate (PMID 37669412) did
not parse as a standard article, which is consistent with a Cochrane record type but is not
proof. **Never guess an identifier**; this needs a human to name it, and then the query runs
unchanged.
