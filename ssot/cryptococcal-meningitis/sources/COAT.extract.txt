COAT -- Timing of Antiretroviral Therapy after Diagnosis of Cryptococcal Meningitis
PMID 24963568 | PMCID PMC4127879 | DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1312884
N Engl J Med, 26 June 2014. Cryptococcal Optimal ART Timing Trial.
ClinicalTrials.gov registration: NCT01075152
Retrieved from PubMed Central full text (open access author manuscript).

WHAT THIS TRIAL RANDOMISES
  NOT an antifungal comparison. Every participant received the SAME
  antifungal induction: amphotericin B 0.7-1.0 mg/kg/day + fluconazole
  800 mg/day for 14 days, then consolidation fluconazole.
  The randomised contrast is the TIMING OF ANTIRETROVIRAL THERAPY.
    earlier-ART  : ART within 48 h of randomisation
                   (median 9 days from meningitis diagnosis, IQR 8-9)
    deferred-ART : ART 4 weeks after randomisation
                   (median 36 days from diagnosis, IQR 34-38)
  ==> This trial shares NO treatment node with an antifungal-induction
      network. It cannot enter such a network as an edge. It is a
      separate two-arm comparison of a different intervention class.

POPULATION AND CONDUCT
  Mulago Hospital (Kampala) and Mbarara Hospital, Uganda; GF Jooste
  Hospital, Cape Town, South Africa. Enrolment from Nov 2010 / Feb 2011 /
  Apr 2011 respectively. HIV-positive ART-naive adults with cryptococcal
  meningitis, entering after 7-11 days of antifungal treatment
  (median 8 days, IQR 7-8).
  389 screened, 177 randomised. Total follow-up 100.7 person-years.
  Vital status at 46 weeks known for all 177 except 1 who withdrew consent.
  Flucytosine was NOT used -- unavailable in the setting.

STOPPED EARLY
  Enrolment stopped 27 April 2012 by the NIAID data and safety monitoring
  board after the second interim review, for substantial EXCESS MORTALITY
  with earlier ART. Intended sample size was 500; 177 were enrolled.
  Monitoring used a Lan-DeMets spending-function analogue of O'Brien-
  Fleming boundaries.
  The authors' own caution, verbatim: "Historically, trials stopped early
  have routinely overestimated the magnitude of benefit or harm."

PRIMARY END POINT
  Survival at 26 weeks, analysed as time-to-event with a Cox
  proportional-hazards model, intention-to-treat, two-sided alpha 0.05.
  (Reported in the text as mortality at 26 weeks.)

PRIMARY RESULT (26-week mortality)
  earlier-ART  : 40 / 88 died = 45%
  deferred-ART : 27 / 89 died = 30%
  hazard ratio for death 1.73, 95% CI 1.06 to 2.82, P = 0.03
  DIRECTION: death is the outcome and earlier ART is the index arm, so a
  hazard ratio ABOVE one means earlier ART is WORSE. This trial's finding
  is HARM from the index strategy.

TIMING OF THE EXCESS
  Days 8-30 (2 to 5 weeks after diagnosis), during consolidation
  fluconazole 800 mg/day:
    earlier-ART  : 21 / 75 = 28%
    deferred-ART :  8 / 80 = 10%
    hazard ratio 3.10, 95% CI 1.37 to 7.00, P = 0.007
  Mortality did not differ significantly thereafter (P = 0.87).
  46-week overall hazard ratio 1.66, 95% CI 1.03 to 2.68, P = 0.04.
  Between weeks 26 and 46 there was 1 further death in the earlier-ART
  group and 2 in the deferred-ART group.

SUBGROUPS (prespecified, interaction terms)
  The ONLY characteristic with a significant interaction was the CSF
  white-cell count at randomisation (P = 0.02 for interaction).
    CSF WCC < 5 /mm3  : HR 3.87, 95% CI 1.41 to 10.58, P = 0.008
    CSF WCC >= 5 /mm3 : HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.46 to 1.71, P = 0.71
  Other subgroups (no significant interaction):
    CD4 < 50 /mm3            : HR 1.74, 95% CI 0.93 to 3.24
    altered mental status
      (Glasgow Coma Scale <15): HR 2.96, 95% CI 1.00 to 8.80
  Earlier ART was not favourable in ANY subgroup analysed.

CRYPTOCOCCAL IRIS -- THE EXACT PUBLISHED FIGURES
  Cumulative incidence of recognised cryptococcal IRIS, compared using the
  cumulative incidence function TO ACCOUNT FOR THE COMPETING RISK OF DEATH:
    earlier-ART  : 17 / 87 = 20%
    deferred-ART :  9 / 69 = 13%
    P = 0.32  (not significant)
  NOTE THE DENOMINATORS: 87 and 69, NOT the ITT 88 and 89. They are the
  at-risk sets for the competing-risk analysis and must not be swapped
  for the ITT denominators.
  NO ratio effect measure (no OR, RR or HR) is published for IRIS.
  ==> A cell reading "17 vs 7" is wrong on the deferred-arm COUNT (it is 9)
      and on both denominators. An odds ratio of 2.50 for IRIS appears
      NOWHERE in this paper and is not derivable from a competing-risk
      cumulative incidence. Such a cell must be removed, not corrected,
      because the trial published a P value and two incidences only.
  IRIS events were adjudicated by an external panel of three physicians
  unaware of ART timing, using the published case definition. The authors
  state the definition's requirement for prior clinical improvement
  probably caused ASCERTAINMENT BIAS with underdetection of early IRIS
  events in the earlier-ART group.

OTHER SECONDARY END POINTS -- all null
  early fungicidal activity : -0.31 log CFU/mL/day in BOTH groups
                              (95% CI -0.33 to -0.28; 166 participants)
  CSF culture positive at 14 days : 37% earlier vs 39% deferred, P = 0.87
  among 59 still culture-positive at 14 days, median growth
                              100 CFU/mL (IQR 15 to 500), P = 0.13
  adverse events            : frequent, mostly attributable to
                              amphotericin, no significant difference
  Verbatim: "The timing of ART initiation did not influence any of the
  secondary end points."

CSF WHITE-CELL RESPONSE
  At day 14 of amphotericin, proportion with CSF WCC >= 5 /mm3:
    58% earlier-ART vs 40% deferred-ART, P = 0.047
  Among those without pleocytosis at diagnosis, an influx occurred in
  10 / 23 (43%) earlier-ART vs 0 / 20 deferred-ART, P = 0.001.

CAUSES OF DEATH (earlier-ART, deferred-ART)
  cryptococcal meningitis related : 19, 10
  bacterial sepsis                :  9,  7
  tuberculosis related            :  2,  2
  IRIS related                    :  1,  2
  cryptococcal meningitis relapse :  0,  2
  medication adverse event        :  2,  0
  pulmonary embolism              :  2,  2
  hypokalaemia or hypovolaemia    :  0,  1
  unknown                         :  2,  3
  earlier-ART only: toxoplasmosis 1, intracranial pressure 1,
                    head trauma 1, multifactorial 1
  27 postmortem examinations (39% of deaths). External adjudication agreed
  with 88% of clinician-ascertained cryptococcal causes of death.
  13 cryptococcal-related deaths occurred 2-5 weeks after diagnosis
  (10 earlier-ART, 3 deferred-ART) and were judged to be caused by the
  INITIAL cryptococcosis rather than by distinct IRIS events. The authors
  state it is "clinically unclear" whether the excess deaths were due to
  sequelae of cryptococcal meningitis or to IRIS.

FUNDING
  National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases and others.
  PEPFAR supported HIV care but not research. Merck Sharp & Dohme donated
  a reserve supply of efavirenz and had no other involvement.
