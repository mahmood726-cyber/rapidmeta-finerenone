ARTEMISININ-BASED COMBINATION THERAPIES -- verbatim extracts.
Every cell in the object is read from a sentence below.

The outcome throughout is PCR-CORRECTED TREATMENT FAILURE in the PER-PROTOCOL
population, at each trial's own prespecified end of follow-up. The papers report
its complement, the adequate clinical and parasitological response, so a failure
count below is the evaluable total minus the response count, and the arithmetic
is shown wherever that is done.

================================================================================
GUINEA-BISSAU -- dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine against artemether-lumefantrine
Ursing J, Rombo L, Rodrigues A, Kofoed P-E (2016). "Artemether-Lumefantrine
versus Dihydroartemisinin-Piperaquine for Treatment of Uncomplicated Plasmodium
falciparum Malaria in Children Aged Less than 15 Years in Guinea-Bissau -- An
Open-Label Non-Inferiority Randomised Clinical Trial." PLoS ONE 11(9): e0161495.
PMID 27649561   DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0161495   Registration NCT01704508
Open access.
================================================================================

Site: the Bandim Health and Demographic Surveillance Site in suburban Bissau,
with treatment at Bandim and Belem Health Centres.

Randomisation: 312 children in total, 155 to artemether-lumefantrine and 157 to dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine.

Day 42, per-protocol, PCR-adjusted:
  dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine: 136 adequate clinical and parasitological responses out of 136 evaluable (100%)
  artemether-lumefantrine: 128 out of 135 evaluable (95%)

So PCR-corrected treatment FAILURES at day 42 are 136 - 136 = 0 of 136 for dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine, and 135 - 128 = 7 of 135 for artemether-lumefantrine.

Follow-up: 42 days. This is the LONGEST follow-up of the three trials here.

CAMEROON -- artesunate-amodiaquine against artemether-lumefantrine
Niba PTN et al (2022). "Effectiveness and safety of artesunate-amodiaquine
versus artemether-lumefantrine for home-based treatment of uncomplicated
Plasmodium falciparum malaria among children 6-120 months in Yaounde, Cameroon:
a randomized trial." BMC Infect Dis 22: 166.
PMID 35189818   DOI 10.1186/s12879-022-07101-2   Registration NCT04565184
Open access.
================================================================================

Design, verbatim: "A two-arm, open-label, randomized, controlled trial comparing
the equivalence of AS-AQ (experimental group) and AL (control group) was carried
out from May 2019 to April 2020 at two secondary hospitals in Yaounde."

THE DOSING IS NOT SUPERVISED, verbatim: "After the first dose, antimalarial
drugs were given at home, rather than under direct observation by a study staff.
The conventional on-treatment and post-treatment laboratory and clinical
evaluations were not done until day 3 of the full antimalarial treatment course."

The paper describes what it measures as EFFECTIVENESS rather than efficacy, and
its title says home-based treatment. The other two trials here administered
treatment under observation.

Randomisation, verbatim: "A total of 242 children were randomized to receive
AS-AQ (n = 114) and AL (n = 128)."

Day 28, per-protocol, PCR-adjusted, verbatim: "The PP PCR adjusted day 28 cure
rates were [AS-AQ = 96.9% (95% CI, 91.2-99.4) versus AL = 95.5% (95% CI,
89.9-98.5), P = 0.797]."

The per-protocol evaluable totals are smaller than the randomised totals.
A cure rate of 96.9 per cent corresponds to 94 of 97 evaluable, that is 3 failures of 97; a cure rate of 95.5 per cent corresponds to 107 of 112, that is 5 failures of 112.
Those are the evaluable denominators this object stores.

Follow-up: 28 days.

Note on a site error in an earlier record: this trial ran at two hospitals in
Yaounde, Cameroon. It has no connection to Garki, which is in Nigeria.

NIGERIA -- pyronaridine-artesunate against artemether-lumefantrine
Falade CO et al (2023). "Efficacy and safety of pyronaridine-artesunate versus
artemether-lumefantrine in the treatment of acute uncomplicated malaria in
children in South-West Nigeria: an open-labelled randomized controlled trial."
Malaria Journal 22: 154.
PMID 37179349   PMC10182553   DOI 10.1186/s12936-023-04574-7
Registration NCT05192265.  Open access.
================================================================================

Site and size, verbatim: "In an open-labelled, randomized, controlled clinical
trial, 172 children aged 3-144 months with a history of fever and
microscopically confirmed uncomplicated Plasmodium falciparum malaria were
enrolled in southwest Nigeria." The site is Ibadan.

Allocation, verbatim: "Eighty-seven (50.6%) received AL, while 85 (49.4%)
received PA."

Day 28, per-protocol, PCR-corrected, verbatim: "PCR-corrected Day-28 cure rates
for PA were 97.4% (76/78) and 88.1% (59/67) for AL (= 0.04) in the per-protocol
population after new infections were censored."

So PCR-corrected treatment FAILURES at day 28 are 78 - 76 = 2 of 78 for pyronaridine-artesunate, and 67 - 59 = 8 of 67 for artemether-lumefantrine.

A claim that both arms exceeded 90 per cent cure is FALSE of this trial: the
artemether-lumefantrine arm cured 88.1 per cent.

Follow-up: 28 days.

THE TWO REGISTRATIONS AWAITING RESULTS
================================================================================

NCT06076213, "Test Efficacy Study on the Recommended Antimalarial Drugs in the
Democratic Republic of the Congo": the registry record carries NO results
section. Its overall status is completed.

NCT04767191, "Malaria Therapeutic Efficacy Study (TES) Kenya": the registry
record carries NO results section. Its overall status is completed.

Neither registration posts outcome data, and no publication reporting either was
located. Any per-arm counts attributed to them are therefore unsourced and are
not carried. The studies themselves are NOT excluded: they are recorded as
awaiting classification, contributing no data, so that they remain visible in
the evidence map until results exist to read.
