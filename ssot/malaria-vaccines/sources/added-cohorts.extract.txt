FRACTIONAL AND DELAYED DOSE PHASE 2b -- Ghana and Kenya
PMID: 40975080   Lancet Global Health 2025 (final, 50-month)
Earlier report of the SAME cohort: PMID 35753316, Lancet Infect Dis 2022
Registration: NCT03276962
================================================================================

[DESIGN AND THE SHARED CONTROL]
"This open-label, randomised controlled trial was conducted at two research
centres in Agogo (Ghana) and Siaya County (Kenya) between Sept 28, 2017, and Nov
14, 2022. Children aged 5-17 months were randomly assigned (1:1:1:1:1) to one of
five groups to receive rabies vaccine (the control group) at months 0, 1, and 2;
or full doses of RTS,S at months 0, 1, and 2, followed by either full doses (R)
at month 20 (group R012-20) or months 14, 26, and 38 (R012-14-26-38); or full
doses at months 0 and 1, followed by fractional doses (Fx; one-fifth of full
dose) at months 2, 14, 26, and 38 (Fx012-14-26-38) or months 7, 20, and 32
(Fx017-20-32)."

"1609 were randomly assigned (322 to each RTS,S group and 321 to the control
group). Of these 1609 children, 1500 received at least one study vaccine dose
(exposed set), and 1333 were included in the per-protocol set for efficacy."

FOUR vaccinated groups against ONE control group of 321 children. Four published
contrasts, one shared control.

[THE ESTIMAND -- IDENTICAL TO THE PHASE 3 TRIAL'S]
From the earlier report of this same cohort (PMC9420828), Methods:
"Vaccine efficacy against all episodes of clinical malaria was calculated as
100 x (1 - incidence rate ratio), overall (adjusted for country as a fixed
effect) and by country, and analysed by negative binomial regression allowing
for interdependence between episodes within the same child."

Compare the RTS,S phase 3 trial's own statement, staged separately with this
object: "Efficacy against all episodes of malaria was analysed by negative
binomial regression with follow-up time as offset, allowing for interdependence
between episodes within the same participant. Overall estimates were adjusted
for study site as a fixed effect."

Same measure, same model, same unit of analysis, same allowance for
within-child dependence, same fixed-effect adjustment for site or country.

[THE CASE DEFINITION -- ALSO IDENTICAL TO THE PHASE 3 TRIAL'S]
From the same earlier report of this cohort (PMC9420828), Methods:
"The primary case definition for clinical malaria was P falciparum asexual
parasitaemia more than 5000 parasites per uL and fever (axillary temperature
>=37.5 degrees C) identified during passive detection."

Compare the RTS,S phase 3 trial's own statement, staged separately with this
object: "The primary case definition for clinical malaria was an illness
accompanied by an axillary temperature of at least 37.5 degrees C and asexual
parasitaemia (>5000 parasites per mm3)".

Both require a MEASURED fever at or above the same threshold together with
parasitaemia above the same density. Neither admits a reported history of fever
in place of a measured temperature. This was staged because the object marks
the case definition as identical across the two cohorts it pools, and a
uniformity claim with no source behind it is an assertion rather than a check.

[RESULT -- ALL EPISODES, TO MONTH 50, EXPOSED SET]
"Among children in the exposed set, to month 50, vaccine efficacy against all
episodes of clinical malaria was 36% (95% CI 19-50), 51% (37-61), 43% (28-55),
and 41% (26-53) in groups R012-20, R012-14-26-38, Fx012-14-26-38, and
Fx017-20-32, respectively (p<0.001 for all)."

[WHY THE R012-20 GROUP IS THE ONE POOLED]
R012-20 is three full doses at months 0, 1 and 2 followed by a full booster at
month 20. That is the same schedule as the boosted group of the phase 3 trial,
which received doses at months 0, 1 and 2 and a booster at month 20. The other
three groups received additional or fractional doses on schedules that no other
cohort in this object used. The exposed set -- children who received at least one
study vaccine dose -- is the same population definition the phase 3 trial calls
modified intention-to-treat.

RTS,S/AS01E WITH THE EXPANDED PROGRAMME ON IMMUNISATION -- Ghana, Tanzania, Gabon
PMID: 21782519   Lancet Infectious Diseases 2011
Registration: NCT00436007
================================================================================

[DESIGN AND THE SHARED CONTROL]
"We did a randomised, open-label, phase 2 trial of safety and efficacy of the
RTS,S/AS01(E) candidate malaria vaccine given with EPI vaccines between April 30,
2007, and Oct 7, 2009, in Ghana, Tanzania, and Gabon. Eligible children were 6-10
weeks of age at first vaccination"

"Participants were randomly assigned (1:1:1) to receive three doses of
RTS,S/AS01(E) at 6, 10, and 14 weeks (0, 1, 2 month schedule) or at 6 weeks, 10
weeks, and 9 months (0, 2, 7 month schedule) or placebo."

"511 children were enrolled."

TWO vaccinated schedules against ONE control group.

[RESULT -- 19-MONTH FOLLOW-UP, ACCORDING TO PROTOCOL, SITE-ADJUSTED]
"Vaccine efficacy for the 0, 1, 2 month schedule (2 weeks after dose three to
month 19, site-adjusted according-to-protocol analysis) was 53% (95% CI 26-70;
p=0.0012) against first malaria episodes and 59% (36-74; p=0.0001) against all
malaria episodes."

"For the entire study period, (total vaccinated cohort) vaccine efficacy against
all malaria episodes was higher with the 0, 1, 2 month schedule (57%, 95% CI
33-73; p=0.0002) than with the 0, 1, 7 month schedule (32% CI 16-45; p=0.0003)."

[WHY THIS COHORT IS NOT POOLED WITH ANY OTHER]
Its follow-up runs to month 19, which is shorter than the three-to-four-year
windows of the other all-episode rows and longer than the twelve-month windows of
the time-to-first rows. Its participants were 6-10 weeks old at first
vaccination, and it is open-label where the other infant cohort is
observer-blind. The 0, 1, 2 month schedule is the one carried forward to phase 3
and is the contrast used here; the 0, 1, 7 month schedule shares this cohort's
control group and is carried outside every pool.
