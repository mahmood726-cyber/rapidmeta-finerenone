SOURCE: Datoo MS et al. "Efficacy of a low-dose candidate malaria vaccine, R21 in
adjuvant Matrix-M, with seasonal administration to children in Burkina Faso: a
randomised controlled trial." Lancet 2021.
PMCID: PMC8121760   PMID: 33964223   DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00943-0
Registration: NCT03896724
Retrieved via PubMed Central full text.

VERBATIM EXTRACTS -- these are the sentences the object's cells were read from.

[DESIGN]
"This was a phase 2b, randomised, controlled, double-blind trial done at the
Institut de Recherche en Sciences de la Sante, Nanoro, Burkina Faso.
Participants aged 5-17 months were recruited"

"Eligible participants were recruited into three groups. Group 1 received 5 ug
R21/25 ug MM, group 2 received 5 ug R21/50 ug MM, and group 3, the control
group, received rabies vaccinations."

"Children aged 5-17 months were randomly assigned (1:1:1) to groups 1-3."

"A rabies vaccine (Rabivax-S), manufactured by the Serum Institute of India, was
the control vaccine."

[ESTIMAND -- THIS IS A TIME-TO-EVENT ANALYSIS, NOT A BINARY ONE]
"Cox regression models were used to analyse first episodes of clinical malaria
from 14 days after the third vaccination to 6 months and 1 year. For
participants without an episode of clinical malaria, their time was censored at
the date of their withdrawal or the date of their 6-month or 12-month blood
sampling."

"Vaccine efficacy was calculated as 1 minus the hazard ratio (HR)."

"The primary comparisons were prespecified as being between groups 1 and 3 and
groups 2 and 3, with comparison of groups 1 and 2 to 3 as a supplementary
analysis."

"Primary analyses of vaccine efficacy were based on a modified intention-to-treat
population, which included all participants who received three vaccinations,
allowing for inclusion of participants who received the wrong vaccine at any
timepoint. Because all vaccines were administered correctly, this is equivalent
to a per-protocol analysis."

[NOTE ON A REPAIR TO THIS EXTRACT]
The sentence above was previously truncated after "who received three
vaccinations". The object asserted, on the strength of it, that this trial
states its modified intention-to-treat analysis is equivalent to per-protocol.
That assertion is true of the paper and was NOT supported by what had been
staged, which a gate reviewer found by reading the staged text rather than the
paper. The full passage is restored here so the claim rests on the evidence
beside it.

[CASE DEFINITION]
"The primary case definition of clinical malaria was presence of an axillary
temperature of 37.5 degrees C or higher and asexual parasite density of more
than 5000 parasites per uL."

[PRIMARY 6-MONTH RESULT]
"These cases of clinical malaria occurred in 43 (29%) of 146 participants in
group 1, 38 (26%) of 146 participants in group 2, and 105 (71%) of 147
participants in group 3. A Cox regression model comparing group 1 with group 3
resulted in vaccine efficacy of 74% (95% CI 63-82; p<0.0001). Comparing group 2
with group 3 resulted in 77% efficacy (67-84; p<0.0001)."

[12-MONTH RESULT -- THE ONE USED IN THIS OBJECT'S PRIMARY POOL]
"Efficacy was further assessed at 12 months (range 329-369 days) after the third
vaccination. 195 participants had clinical malaria -- an increase of nine
participants from the primary 6-month analysis (the period of low malaria
transmission). These cases occurred in 50 (34%) of 146 participants in group 1,
39 (27%) of 146 participants in group 2, and 106 (72%) of 147 participants in
group 3. Cox regression showed vaccine efficacy of 71% (95% CI 59-79; p<0.0001)
for group 1, and 77% (67-84; p<0.0001) for group 2."

[NOTE ON AN INTERNAL DISCREPANCY IN THIS PAPER]
The ABSTRACT of this paper states: "At 1 year, vaccine efficacy remained high, at
77% (67-84) in group 1." The RESULTS section quoted immediately above states the
1-year figures as 71% (59-79) for group 1 and 77% (67-84) for group 2. The two
statements disagree about which group the 1-year 77% belongs to. This object
uses the RESULTS section, which reports both groups explicitly and is internally
consistent with the group-2 6-month figure.

[SEASONAL ADMINISTRATION]
"Doses were administered before the seasonal peak of malaria transmission
starting in July."

"To ensure antibody responses were highest during the seasonal peak of malaria
transmission, resulting in potentially increased vaccine efficacy, we
administered three doses in the primary vaccination series largely before the
malaria season."

[ENROLMENT]
"From May 7 to June 13, 2019, 498 children aged 5-17 months were screened. 48
children were excluded, leaving 450 children who were enrolled and received at
least one vaccination. 150 children were allocated to 5 ug R21 plus 25 ug MM
(group 1), 150 children were allocated to 5 ug R21 plus 50 ug MM (group 2), and
150 children were allocated to the control vaccine (group 3)."

[SHARED CONTROL -- LOAD-BEARING FOR THE DOUBLE-COUNTING RULE]
Groups 1 and 2 are two different adjuvant doses compared against ONE control
group (group 3, n=147 in the 12-month analysis). Both published contrasts use the
same control participants. Using both contrasts in one pooled estimate would
count those control participants twice.
