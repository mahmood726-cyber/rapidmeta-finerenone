BEJON 2008 -- RTS,S/AS01E phase 2b, Kenya and Tanzania
PMID: 19064627   PMCID: PMC2655100   DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa0807381
Registration: NCT00380393
N Engl J Med 2008; 359(24): 2521-32
================================================================================

"We conducted a double-blind, randomized trial of RTS,S/AS01E vaccine as compared
with rabies vaccine in children in Kilifi, Kenya, and Korogwe, Tanzania. The
primary end point was fever with a falciparum parasitemia density of more than
2500 parasites per microliter, and the mean duration of follow-up was 7.9 months
(range, 4.5 to 10.5)."

"A total of 894 children were randomly assigned to receive the RTS,S/AS01E vaccine
or the control (rabies) vaccine. Among the 809 children who completed the study
procedures according to the protocol, the cumulative number in whom clinical
malaria developed was 32 of 402 assigned to receive RTS,S/AS01E and 66 of 407
assigned to receive the rabies vaccine; the adjusted efficacy rate for
RTS,S/AS01E was 53% (95% confidence interval [CI], 28 to 69; P<0.001) on the
basis of Cox regression. Overall, there were 38 episodes of clinical malaria
among recipients of RTS,S/AS01E, as compared with 86 episodes among recipients of
the rabies vaccine, with an adjusted rate of efficacy against all malarial
episodes of 56% (95% CI, 31 to 72; P<0.001). All 894 children were included in
the intention-to-treat analysis, which showed an unadjusted efficacy rate of 49%
(95% CI, 26 to 65; P<0.001)."

WHY NOT POOLED: the case definition threshold is a parasitaemia density of more
than 2500 parasites per microlitre, whereas every other trial in this object uses
a threshold of more than 5000. The follow-up window is a mean of 7.9 months, not
12 months. Both differences change what is being estimated.

CHANDRAMOHAN 2021 -- RTS,S/AS01E seasonal vaccination, Burkina Faso and Mali
PMID: 34432975   DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2026330
Registration: NCT03143218
N Engl J Med 2021; 385(11): 1005-1017
================================================================================

"We conducted an individually randomized, controlled trial to assess whether
seasonal vaccination with RTS,S/AS01 was noninferior to chemoprevention in
preventing uncomplicated malaria and whether the two interventions combined were
superior to either one alone in preventing uncomplicated malaria and severe
malaria-related outcomes."

"We randomly assigned 6861 children 5 to 17 months of age to receive
sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine and amodiaquine (2287 children [chemoprevention-alone
group]), RTS,S/AS01 (2288 children [vaccine-alone group]), or chemoprevention and
RTS,S/AS01 (2286 children [combination group])."

"There were 305 events of uncomplicated clinical malaria per 1000 person-years at
risk in the chemoprevention-alone group, 278 events per 1000 person-years in the
vaccine-alone group, and 113 events per 1000 person-years in the combination
group. The hazard ratio for the protective efficacy of RTS,S/AS01 as compared
with chemoprevention was 0.92 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.84 to 1.01), which
excluded the prespecified noninferiority margin of 1.20."

"Administration of RTS,S/AS01 was noninferior to chemoprevention in preventing
uncomplicated malaria."

WHY NOT POOLED: the comparator is ACTIVE -- seasonal malaria chemoprevention with
sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine and amodiaquine -- not a placebo or an unrelated
control vaccine. The hazard ratio quoted above is vaccine versus chemoprevention.
It is not a vaccine efficacy against an unprotected control and it does not
estimate the same quantity as any other row in this object. Reading it as a
vaccine efficacy would report this vaccine as having almost no effect.

DATOO 2022 -- SECOND PUBLICATION OF THE DATOO 2021 COHORT
PMID: 36087586   Lancet Infect Dis 2022
Registration: NCT03896724 -- THE SAME REGISTRATION AS DATOO 2021
================================================================================

Title: "Efficacy and immunogenicity of R21/Matrix-M vaccine against clinical
malaria after 2 years' follow-up in children in Burkina Faso: a phase 1/2b
randomised controlled trial."

The Datoo 2024 phase 3 paper describes this publication as reporting the same
cohort: "We previously reported 24 month efficacy of 75% and 77% for time to
first episode and multiple episodes of clinical malaria, respectively, with four
doses of R21/Matrix-M in children aged 5-17 months at enrolment in Nanoro,
Burkina Faso, in a phase 2b study."

WHY NOT A SEPARATE STUDY: this is a longer follow-up of the SAME 450 randomised
children reported in Datoo 2021 under the same registration. Counting it as an
independent study would enter one randomised cohort into a synthesis twice.

2015 final report used here. NCT00380393 (Bejon 2008) has later follow-up

already represented in this object, and none is counted again.
