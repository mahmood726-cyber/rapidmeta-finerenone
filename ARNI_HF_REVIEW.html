The I-squared here is the Higgins statistic computed from Q and its degrees of freedom, and it falls in the lowest band of section 10.10.2. That band is reported with a caveat rather than as reassurance: with three trials, two of which have wide intervals, a low I-squared reflects imprecision as much as agreement, and the point estimates themselves range from 0.80 to 1.09 and straddle the null. The uniformity table and the leave-one-out analysis are where that spread is visible; the I-squared alone would hide it.

The methods rule governing this decision

Decision: POOL three trials reporting the same effect measure for the same outcome concept against the same comparator, producing an AVERAGE ACROSS documented differences in aetiology, ejection-fraction ceiling, blinding and the endpoint's rank within its own trial

Cochrane Handbook sections: 3.2.1, 3.2.3, 3.2.4, 10.9, 10.10.1, 10.10.2, 10.10.4, 10.14

Sections 3.2.1, 3.2.3 and 3.2.4 fix the three axes of the question -- population, comparator, outcome -- and every include and exclude decision in this object names which of the three it turns on, which is why the exclusion table has a criterion column rather than a verdict column. Section 10.9 gives the hazard ratio as the effect measure for time-to-event data and sanctions combining log hazard ratios by generic inverse variance; all three trials report a Cox hazard ratio for the time to a first event of the same composite against enalapril, so the measure is shared and not converted. What the pool crosses is documented under 10.10.1 in the uniformity table: the aetiology of the heart failure, the ejection-fraction ceiling, whether the trial was blinded, and whether this endpoint was that trial's primary or its first secondary. Not one of those is smoothed away and none is claimed to be absent. Section 10.10.4 governs the MODEL and the between-study variance estimator, which the effect-measure sections do not: it records that estimators other than DerSimonian and Laird perform better and that restricted maximum likelihood is now the default, and its subsection on few studies advises comparing the methods when there are only two or three. This pool has three, so the comparison is RUN and sits in the sensitivity block, where the three estimators do not agree closely and that is shown rather than reported as a formality. Section 10.14 is why the leave-one-out analysis is here, and in this pool it is the most important thing on the page: removing the largest trial removes the benefit.

Leave-one-out sensitivity

Two decisions. First, whether the pooled benefit depends on any single trial -- and it does. Second, whether it depends on the choice of between-study variance estimator and interval method, which with three studies is not a formality.

The pooled benefit rests on PARADIGM-HF, and the table is blunter than that summary sounds. Every leave-one-out fit still points below the null, so the DIRECTION is stable. The INTERVAL is not: only the fit that drops PARALLEL-HF keeps the null outside it. Dropping PARADIGM-HF or dropping PARACHUTE-HF both leave an interval that includes the null, so two of the three fits do. That is the single most important qualification on the headline, it is what the imprecision domain of the GRADE assessment is counting, and it is stated here rather than left for a reader to derive from the table.

Cochrane Handbook version 6.5.1, section 10.14: sensitivity analyses examine whether overall findings are robust to potentially influential decisions. Section 10.10.4.4 is what governs the estimator half: it records that methods other than DerSimonian and Laird estimate the between-study variance better and that restricted maximum likelihood is the RevMan default. An earlier version of this field credited that advice to section 10.10.4.5, which does not give it -- 10.10.4.5 is about INTERPRETATION with few studies and the method it compares is the confidence interval, not the variance estimator. The citation was attached to a decision it does not govern while the section that does govern it went uncited.

Does the answer depend on the pooling method?

Section 10.10.4.4 records that estimators other than DerSimonian and Laird perform better and that restricted maximum likelihood is now the default in the Cochrane software, and it is the whole of the authority for running this comparison. Nothing in the Handbook instructs a review to compare variance estimators at a particular number of studies; running it here is a choice made under section 10.14, because with a pool this small the choice is plausibly influential. It is: unlike the two-study pools elsewhere in this batch, the estimators here do NOT coincide.

The three variance estimators give visibly different between-study variances and therefore different intervals, with restricted maximum likelihood the most conservative of the three; all three keep the interval clear of the null. The Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman interval does NOT: it is much wider and it includes the null. Section 10.10.4.5 says to expect exactly that with so few studies, so the disagreement is not treated as one method being wrong. Both are printed and neither is decisive, and the headline is reported as an estimate whose interval excludes the null under the ordinary method and does not under the more conservative one. The floor on the Hartung-Knapp scale factor is applied, so that interval can widen and can never come out narrower than the ordinary one.

Restricted maximum likelihood is kept as the headline estimator, with the ordinary interval. Section 10.10.4.4 records that it is now the default in the Cochrane software and that estimators other than DerSimonian and Laird perform better; it is also the most conservative of the three here, so keeping it is not a choice that flatters the result. The Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman interval is reported beside it rather than instead of it, and the fact that the two disagree about the null is stated in the headline rather than left in this table.

What the search found, and what was kept

The search ran across PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov for randomised comparisons of sacubitril/valsartan with enalapril in heart failure, and was then extended by the reconciliation step: the included-study list of every retrievable published synthesis of the same comparison was read and diffed against this one in both directions. That second pass is what found PARALLEL-HF, which the brief this object was built from did not name.

Eligible: A randomised trial of sacubitril/valsartan against enalapril, in adults with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction, reporting cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization as a time-to-first-event hazard ratio. All four conditions, and the last is a condition on the QUANTITY reported, not on the trial's quality: several excluded trials are large, well conducted and directly on topic, and report something that is not this.
