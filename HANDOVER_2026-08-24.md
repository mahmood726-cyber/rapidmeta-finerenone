## Why six rounds failed, and what broke it open

Every previous round — and most of today — tested only the pages **suspected of being bad**.
Each round found real defects on those pages, fixed them, measured them to zero on those
pages, and reported success. The measurement was true and the conclusion was wrong.

The thing that broke it open was a **control**: running the blind reader panel on a page
with *real pooled evidence* (`SGLT2_MACE_CVOT_REVIEW`, 2 trials, OR 0.9074, 26,453
characters of prose). It read just as badly:

> *"the core question is a corrupted, truncated outcome-label dump; major sections are
> placeholders"* — Codex/GPT-5
> *"a nearly unreadable structural failure"* — Gemini 3.1 Pro

**The defect was never about empty pages.** It is in the manuscript layer itself, and it
affects the pages with evidence too. Everything fixed today was aimed at the empty ones.

> **Whatever the next design is, its acceptance test needs a known-good comparator from
> the start.** A suite that only examines suspected-bad cases cannot tell you that your
> diagnosis is too narrow. That is the single most transferable thing on this page.

The 1,309 were probed for every defect class found today: **0 carry any**. They are excluded
because they have no manuscript, *not* because they passed. Any claim of "fixed" needs that
denominator attached.

Within the 162 mapped topics, measured by
`scripts/measure_poolability_2026_08_24.py` (threshold: ≥2 distinct trials by registration
ID, one shared measure, point and both bounds present and read not computed):

| group | count | state | artefact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 29 | live pooled estimate | manuscript |
| **B** | 20 | readable per-trial estimates, **pool withdrawn** | manuscript |
| **C** | 113 | no two readable trials sharing a measure | **structured statement** |

**B is the important distinction and it was nearly got wrong.** The original threshold
separated "has a pooled number" from "doesn't", which would have converted B. But a
withdrawn pool is a page that *has* evidence and declined to combine it, usually for a
substantive reason. Converting those would say "we found nothing" where the truth is "we
found this and chose not to pool it, for this reason" — not a smaller claim, a false one.
The B pages may be the most valuable in the corpus: they publish the unflattering row
instead of quietly pooling. Their withdrawal reason should read as a **finding**, not an
apology.

## The reader panel — what it is and how to run it

Blind: each reader is told it is a peer reviewer for a clinical journal that has never seen
the software, and is given **only the rendered Paper-panel prose**. Prompt is
`scratchpad/read_head.txt`; extraction is `scratchpad/extract_prose.py`.

**Two families, and this matters.** Eight lanes of one family is one opinion sampled eight
times. Codex/GPT-5 runs on the laptop via `F:/E156/outputs/codex_laptop.sh`; Gemini 3.1 Pro
runs **locally** via `agy --print` (the laptop's `agy` is NOT authenticated over SSH — it
launches an interactive OAuth flow that times out; the local one is signed in and returns
`OK Gemini 3.1 Pro`). Gemini is the harsher family: it rated the pre-redesign page
*"Abysmal"* where Codex said *"Badly"*.

Standing score: **0 of 8** on the original, **0 of 5** on the collapsed manuscript,
**0 of 5** on the converted statement, **0 of 3** on the group-A control. Both families
converge; there has been no split to adjudicate.

Both families independently prescribe the same next step:

> *"Strip out all references to the software's internal data structures and missing
> variables, and state only the actual clinical methods and findings — even if that reduces
> the entire paper to three sentences."*

## Five instrument failures, all mine, all fixed

Every one produced a confident finding that was about my tooling, not the system. Named
because the pattern matters more than the individual slips: **I built instruments faster
than I validated them.**

1. **0-byte log.** `python -u` does not reach a `TextIOWrapper` the script installs itself;
   it is block-buffered. A 163-page job reported nothing for 47 minutes. Another lane read
   the empty log, divided the correct rate by the wrong denominator (1,473 instead of 163)
   and reported a **15.7-hour ETA for a 90-minute job**. Fixed with `write_through=True`,
   plus a `progress` line inside the ledger JSON — the durable artefact, not just the stream.
2. **`kill -0` on a Windows PID** from Git Bash returns a false negative. I nearly reported
   a healthy job as crashed at 109/163. Use `Get-Process`.
3. **`ex.map` yields in submission order**, so one 30-minute page (`MALARIA_VACCINES`, 7.5 MB)
   froze the progress file at 109 while three workers had raced to the R's. Now
   `as_completed`.
4. **A backspace byte in source.** `\b` written through a shell heredoc arrived as literal
   `0x08`; the regex matched nothing and the function returned its input unchanged while
   `inspect.getsource` printed code that looked correct. `.githooks/pre-push` runs
   `lint_control_chars.py` for exactly this, and its own comment records the class recurring
   **eight times in one night**. This was the ninth. **Do not write regexes through
   heredocs** — use the editor.
5. **The reader panel judged artefacts I injected.** A 9,000-char cap truncated an
   11,156-char paper (a reviewer cited *"PRISMA 2020 is the g"* as evidence of truncation),
   and slicing `id="pn-paper"` → `end-paper` leaked `id="pn-paper">` and `<!--` into every
   payload (a reviewer reported *"raw HTML leaks into the page"*). Both fixed in
   `extract_prose.py`, which now refuses to truncate silently.

## Two pages held out — both authorised, neither done

**`malaria-vaccines` — `CHK018_MIXED_POOLING`.** An `exploratory_recurrent_rate` pool
combines an **HR** (`datoo-2024-phase3`) with an **IRR** (`rtss-phase3-children`). Different
quantities; the check is right. Pre-existing — fails identically before and after today's
changes — but surfaced only because closing the PAGE_MAP gap un-hid a page every rollout had
silently skipped. Authorised approach: **split the pool by measure** so like pools with
like; if a stratum is then too small to pool, **drop it and state the reason on the page**.
**Do not convert one measure into the other** — that manufactures a number. The page must
say what changed and why, because it alters a previously published claim.
Currently reverted to its exact pre-2026-08-24 state and removed from PAGE_MAP
(163 → 162) so it shipped unchanged rather than half-repaired. **Held out is not fixed and
not worse**: it still carries today's other defects.

**`BOCOCIZUMAB_LIPID_REVIEW.html` — the orphan.** A tabbed page with a Paper panel, an empty
refusal and three spliced sentinels, and **no SSOT object**. The only page in all 1,473
outside the 163 carrying any defect. Authorised: **reconstruct the object only if the
evidence is genuinely poolable** — ≥2 independent trials by registration ID, a shared
outcome measured comparably, estimates readable verbatim. If not poolable, **withdraw the
page**; "not poolable" is the correct outcome, not a failure.

> **The trap, and check it specifically.** The page's own title says the measure was
> corrected in 2026. If that correction changed *what is being measured*, then pre- and
> post-correction figures are **not the same quantity** and pooling them would be wrong
> despite looking poolable. Note that `BOCOCIZUMAB_LIPID_AUTO_FULL_REVIEW` (a different
> page) lands in group A with 6 trials sharing MD on `ldlc_pct_change_wk` — that is
> suggestive but is **not** evidence for the orphan, and six trials sharing a *label* is
> exactly what the trap looks like.

Both change published claims, so both need two model families with a third adjudicating,
numbers read from source and never computed, and the reasoning recorded.

## Gates that stopped me today, and were right every time

The pre-push harness gate has **no override**, correctly. It caught three checks that could
not fail (`CHK021` vacuous on two objects, via a measure vocabulary of `{OR, RR, HR, IRR}`
that knew neither `RATE_RATIO` nor `WIN_RATIO` nor `ratio-type-not-named-in-source`), and one
subtler case worth remembering: `CHK016` reported vacuous on `finerenone-review`, which reads
as "the check ignores `ci_high`" — **it doesn't**. The mutator was a flat `ci_high * 4`, and
on an already-wide interval that is not a flipping value: the row sat at ratio 1.000 and
reached only 1.305 at four times its bound, inside the 1.5 threshold. **The fault was in the
mutation, not the check.** A mutant that cannot flip the verdict proves nothing.

`audit_exclusion_by_absence.py` also refused a per-item skip inside my before/after counting
script — rightly, because a "before" count that silently skips pages under-counts the
baseline and makes the improvement look bigger than it is.
