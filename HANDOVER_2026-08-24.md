# Handover — 2026-08-24

> # 00. A gate only fails in the direction it was built to look.
>
> **The deepest version of the control problem, and it outranks the 56-uncontrolled finding
> — because these controls existed, ran, and passed while the measurement was meaningless.**
>
> 2026-08-25. A data-recovery measurement reported 64 of 116 numbers (55%) recovered from
> ClinicalTrials.gov against a published review's extracted table. It had three controls:
> a positive (a value present in the source classifies EXACT), a negative (a value absent must
> not classify EXACT), and a third state (an empty source yields NOT_POSTED). **All three
> held.**
>
> Then the same 116 numbers were matched against **fourteen trials from unrelated disease
> areas** — anticoagulants, antibiotics, PCSK9, ophthalmology:
>
> | | EXACT |
> |---|---|
> | real trials | 55% |
> | **unrelated trials** | **59%** |
>
> Worse than chance. It had never measured recovery.
>
> **The controls were not wrong. They were answering a different question.** They tested
> whether the *classifier* can tell a matching value from a non-matching one — and it can.
> What they could not test is whether matching against a *pool of every trial in the review*
> means anything, because that is a property of the DESIGN, not of the comparator.
>
> Operationally:
>
> - **A passing control licenses one inference only: the mechanism it exercises works.** It
>   says nothing about whether that mechanism answers the question you asked.
> - Before trusting a rate, ask what the instrument would report **if the phenomenon did not
>   exist at all.** If you cannot say, the controls are not enough.
> - **A NULL TEST IS NOW STANDARD for every recovery-type measurement** — run the instrument
>   against a deliberately wrong comparator and require the real rate to beat it. It belongs
>   in the harness by default, not run when someone remembers.

> # 00a. "Generous to us" is not a caveat. It is a description of a design that cannot fail.
>
> The comment that shipped inside the invalid measurement, written while building it:
>
> > *"That is GENEROUS to us and is stated as such."*
>
> It reads as scrupulous disclosure. It was a design note explaining why the instrument would
> report success whatever the truth — the review names its NCTs but not which row belongs to
> which trial, so every number was matched against every trial's posted values, and with
> counts like 20, 112, 209 something always matches.
>
> **A disclosure that substitutes for a fix is worse than no disclosure**, because it buys the
> credibility of honesty while leaving the defect in place — and it makes the defect harder to
> see afterwards, since the file appears to have considered it.
>
> The rule: **when you find yourself writing that a design is generous to us, stop and check
> whether it can fail at all.** That sentence is the signal to test, not the thing to write.

> # 0. A field that is usually right is more dangerous than one that is always wrong.
>
> **A rule built on a mostly-reliable field passes its examples and fails silently on the
> exceptions.** A field that is always wrong gets noticed on the first test. One that is right
> nine times in ten gets a rule built on it, a green suite, and a defect that only shows up in
> the cases nobody wrote a test for.
>
> The worked example, 2026-08-25. ClinicalTrials.gov registers an arm `type`:
> `EXPERIMENTAL`, `ACTIVE_COMPARATOR`, `PLACEBO_COMPARATOR`. It looks authoritative and it is
> usually correct — PLATO labels ticagrelor `EXPERIMENTAL` and clopidogrel
> `ACTIVE_COMPARATOR`, exactly as you would expect. So a rule was written on it: *the drug
> must sit in an `EXPERIMENTAL` arm*. Then:
>
> | trial | what the label says | what is true |
> |---|---|---|
> | NCT00423319 | `EXPERIMENTAL` = enoxaparin, `ACTIVE_COMPARATOR` = apixaban | the title calls **apixaban** the investigational drug |
> | NCT00468923 | HOPE-3's real rosuvastatin arm is `PLACEBO_COMPARATOR` | a 2×2 factorial labelled from the other factor's point of view |
> | NCT02829957 | RAMBLE: **no** arm is `EXPERIMENTAL` | a head-to-head; both drugs are under test |
>
> **PLATO labels it correctly, which is worse than always wrong, because it invites the rule
> I wrote.** The comparator judgement is now UNDECIDABLE — 16 verdicts given up rather than
> accuse a page on a field that lies sometimes.
>
> **This is a standing question, not a one-off.** Whenever a check is built on a source field,
> ask: *is this field usually right?* If yes, that is a reason for suspicion, not comfort —
> find the cases where it is wrong before shipping, because the rule will not find them for
> you. The registry-first assumption has the same shape: registries are usually right about
> what a trial did, which is exactly why the exceptions have cost this project so much.

> # 0a. A gate that blocks outside its scope teaches people to bypass it.
>
> **A gate's entire value is that it is not bypassed**, so scope errors cost more than missed
> defects. This is an argument for narrow, correct gates over broad, approximately-right ones
> — and it cuts directly against the instinct to make a check catch more.
>
> `lint_control_chars` is the mechanical form of a rule that failed six times as advice, so it
> is the last line against heredoc mangling. It walked the whole worktree. On 2026-08-25 an
> **untracked** scratch artefact — `outputs/_false_refusal_working_table.txt`, written UTF-16
> by a hunt agent, whose NUL bytes are its *encoding* — refused a commit of 149 rebuilt pages.
> Nothing about that file was a defect, and nothing about it was ours.
>
> One such block is all it takes. The next person hits it, reaches for `--no-verify`, and the
> guard is gone for every real case afterwards. Its own docstring already said "a C0 control
> character in a **tracked** text file"; the code had drifted from the contract it documented.
>
> Two consequences worth carrying:
>
> - **Scope a gate to what it is responsible for**, and prefer missing a case to blocking a
>   case that was never in scope. A gate people trust catches more over time than a gate people
>   route around.
> - **A gate's scope is part of its contract** and belongs in its docstring, where drift
>   between the two is visible. Here the docstring was right and the code was wrong, which is
>   the only reason the fix was obvious.

> # 1. A rule derived from the defect that prompted it will be shaped like that defect, not like the class.
>
> **Derive from the class, then check the motivating case falls inside it.** Doing it the
> other way round produces a rule that is provably right about one thing and silently wrong
> about the rest.
>
> The worked example, 2026-08-25. CEFEPIME_TAZ was the case that prompted the trial-identity
> work: the page named a trial of `Cefepime/VNRX-5133` (taniborbactam) for a topic about
> cefepime-**tazobactam**. So the rule was written as *a combination is a different drug from
> its components* — which is exactly true of that case. Run across the corpus it was **wrong
> in 9 of 12 combination cases**, and every error deleted real evidence:
>
> | pattern | trial intervention | the rule said | the truth |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | `sacubitril` | `Sacubitril/valsartan` | not studied | ARNI's own drug |
> | `ceftolozane` | `Ceftolozane/tazobactam` | not studied | only marketed this way |
> | `casirivimab` | `casirivimab+imdevimab` | not studied | only given with imdevimab |
> | `delamanid` | `Delamanid + OBR` | not studied | OBR is background |
> | `apixaban` | `Apixaban + Placebo` | not studied | placebo of the other agent |
>
> The principle that covers all twelve is narrower: a combination **containing** the subject
> drug **is** a trial of that drug; what is not is a combination with a **different partner**
> from the one the topic specifies. Under that rule the motivating case still fails — and
> CEFEPIME_TAZ's real defect relocates to `TOPICS`, which supplies the pattern `cefepime` for
> a drug that is cefepime-tazobactam. **The fix moved layers once the class was described
> properly.** This is the vocabulary-versus-property error at the level of principle rather
> than pattern.

> # 2. Unknown is not no — including when the comparison is against free text.
>
> **A mismatch between a paraphrase and a pattern is not evidence of absence.** This should be
> the default shape of every comparison this project makes against text a human wrote.
>
> The trial-identity rule returned NOT STUDIED whenever no arm name matched the drug pattern.
> That accused **92 of 420 records** on the evidence that two strings differ. Arm names are
> paraphrases: NCT00643188's experimental arm is `"Procedure: Radiofrequency ablation"`
> against the pattern `"catheter ablation"` — the same thing in different words. NCT01420393
> names its arms by *strategy*. Drug arms carry brands, code names and formulations.
>
> The verdict is now **UNDECIDABLE**, a third state that accuses nothing. It **costs coverage
> rather than correctness**, which is the right trade every time: all three decidable failure
> modes require a name match first, so nothing real is lost. The same shape already appears
> as *a fetch failure is not an absence* and *a numerical witness SKIP is not a pass* — this
> is the free-text instance of it, and the one most likely to recur.

> # 3. A changed test expectation carries its justification inline, or it is a suppressed failure.
>
> **Changing a test until it passes is the easiest way to launder a bad rule**, and it looks
> identical in a diff to fixing a wrong expectation. The only thing that distinguishes them is
> the reasoning, so the reasoning is not optional and does not live in a commit message where
> it detaches from the test.
>
> When the combination rule was narrowed, one case flipped from `False` to `True` — a bare
> `cefepime` pattern legitimately matching `Cefepime/VNRX-5133`. That expectation is now
> written in the test file with the twelve corpus cases that forced it, and a second case
> immediately below asserting that the *correct* pattern still rejects, so the original defect
> stays visible rather than being absorbed by the change that fixed the rule.


> # A scan reports where it looked, not a population.
>
> **The single most expensive habit in this repository, and the shortest statement of it we
> have.** Read it before acting on any list of sites a check hands you — including a check
> you wrote and trust, and including a list that came from a real measurement and therefore
> *feels* measured.
>
> It has bitten in three different forms in one week:
>
> | Where | The list said | The population was |
> |---|---|---|
> | The projector's container-repr fix | the sites the scan printed | every block with the property — the fix was applied to three and the page still carried the defect from two more, forty blocks further down |
> | The panel's I² contradiction | 5 pages | **15** — a reviewer flags whichever contradiction it happens to quote |
> | The panel's search-claim contradiction | 1 page | **3** |
>
> And it runs in the **opposite** direction just as often, which is the half that gets
> forgotten: on 2026-08-25 six instruments over-flagged, each manufacturing a defect class
> that did not exist (38→25→15, 97→0, 5→3, and one that would have deleted a true sentence).
> **Over-flagging is not the safe direction.** A manufactured class costs the same review
> time as a real one and sends the fix to the wrong layer.
>
> The rule, operationally: never take the sites a scan named as the population. Re-derive the
> population from the property, sweep it, print the count you swept and the denominator you
> swept it over. This applies to **hand-edits too** — a generator at least applies its logic
> uniformly by construction, whereas a hand-edit applies exactly where the hand went.

> # A reference standard must be audited before it can certify.
>
> **A gate carrying an error is worse than no gate, because it certifies.** Whatever a check
> is validated *against* becomes the thing it enforces — so the reference standard needs the
> same scrutiny as the code, and usually gets none.
>
> **The worked example: RAMBLE (NCT02829957).** The trial-identity sweep labelled apixaban
> the `COMPARATOR` on that trial, and an early version of the matcher test used those labels
> as ground truth. The registry says otherwise:
>
> ```
> ACTIVE_COMPARATOR   Rivaroxaban
> ACTIVE_COMPARATOR   Apixaban
> ```
>
> Neither arm is experimental — it is a head-to-head, and apixaban is the *hypothesised
> better* drug. The sweep reached the **right verdict for the wrong reason**: RAMBLE does not
> belong on an apixaban VTE page because it measures **menstrual blood loss**, not because
> apixaban is a control. Had the matcher been validated against that label, it would have
> learned to reject head-to-head trials — silently dropping real evidence on every
> active-controlled comparison in the corpus, permanently, with a passing test suite.
>
> **Registry interventions describe what was GIVEN, not which arm was EXPERIMENTAL.** That is
> the specific trap here. A drug list cannot tell you the randomised contrast: TWILIGHT lists
> ticagrelor because every participant received it, and the trial randomises aspirin.
>
> Operationally:
>
> - Never validate a check against another check's unverified output. Model verdicts, prior
>   sweeps and harvested claims are **hypotheses**, not standards.
> - A reference standard must be a fact a reader can confirm at source — a registration, a
>   published table, a registry field — and the test should name where to look.
> - When a standard and a rule disagree, audit the standard first. It is the thing nobody
>   checked.

> # A control must be a case that still fails, or the instrument retires itself the moment it succeeds.
>
> **This is the most important rule in this file.** A check whose control is the defect it
> hunts becomes, the day that defect is fixed, a check that certifies silence.
>
> It was earned by an audit doing it to itself. `audit_tidy_blanks_a_value` used a bare
> status word being blanked as its positive control — precisely the defect it existed to
> find. The moment that was fixed the control stopped holding, and the audit **refused to
> print any count** rather than report a clean corpus it could no longer verify. It failed
> closed, which is the good outcome. A check that degrades the other way just starts passing.
>
> **Applied across the repository — 136 `gate_`/`lint_`/`audit_` scripts:**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | use `require_controls` (raises — **fails closed**) | 47 |
> | hand-rolled control mention only (unknown behaviour) | 33 |
> | **no control of any kind — can only ever say yes** | **56** |
>
> The 56 are the finding. Of the 47 safe ones, only two have controls pointing at a live
> corpus instance (`audit_p46_limbs_reach_a_reader`, `audit_why_six_topics_have_no_page`);
> both fail closed, so they are fragile rather than dangerous. A live control is the
> *strongest* evidence a check works on this corpus and the *most* fragile — that is a
> trade-off to make knowingly, not a defect.
>

> # A missing field must never fall through to the answer that flatters us.
>
> **State it this way, because the mildest version convinces nobody:** the same absent value
> produced **INCLUDED** in one renderer and **EXCLUDED** in another, because each default
> flattered its own context. `projectors2.py` defaulted a missing screening decision to
> `"included"` (695 records, 225 of them stored EXCLUDED); `make_docx.py` defaulted the same
> missing field to `"excluded"` (711 of 799 records). One missing field, two renderers,
> opposite answers, both wrong, neither detectable from its own output. That the DOCX erred
> conservatively was luck, not design.
>
> ---
>
> # Neither layer alone is the truth.
>
> The built-versus-served distinction, one level deeper. Verifying the blanking fix **only
> against the projector** would have claimed two pages fixed that were never broken
> (`Meropenem`, `Injection-site swelling` — both group-C pages routed through `statement.py`,
> which never used the projector path). Verifying **only against delivered pages** would have
> looked like failure on those same two. The truth needed both: *does the generator produce
> it*, and *does the page a reader opens contain it*, are different questions with different
> answers.
>
> The worst defect this project has produced was one line:
>
> ```python
> decided = (p(str(r["disposition"])) if r.get("disposition")
>            else ("excluded" if r.get("criteria_failed") else "included"))
> ```
>
> Records written since store their decision in `verdict`. They have neither legacy field,
> so they hit the final `else` and **695 of 799 screening records were displayed as
> "included" when the review had excluded them** — 225 stored `EXCLUDED`, 383
> `NEEDS_ADJUDICATION` — each with its own exclusion reasons printed directly beneath.
>
> The absence of an old-shape key is evidence about our schema. It is not evidence that a
> trial was included. **Same species as absent→zero, unclassifiable→excluded, and a SKIP
> counted as a PASS**: in every one, the fallback is the reassuring answer, and the error is
> therefore invisible to anyone reading the output.
>
> **Audit for other defaults of this shape.** If one existed, others will. The test is not
> "is the default sensible" but "does the default assert something the data does not say".
> Where nothing can be read, say so — this one now renders *"not recorded on this record"*.
>
> Two things it also taught:
>
> - **The bug and its camouflage were the same line.** The old fallback returned the bare
>   literal `"included"` *without* calling the escaper, while the `disposition` branch was
>   escaped. So the first fix — routing the verdict through `p()`, which looked obviously
>   right — replaced 501 wrong decisions with 501 blank ones, because `p()` applies `_tidy`,
>   a *prose* cleaner that drops short fragments: `''` for "excluded", `''` for "included".
>   **A decision is a controlled value, not prose.** Caught by re-reading the rendered page
>   rather than the diff.
> - **A repair must not destroy its own search key.** The dead-stub fixer keyed on
>   `location.replace`, removed it, and thereby made the same 47 pages invisible to a second
>   pass — while their `<meta http-equiv="refresh">` was still bouncing readers to a 404. A
>   fix that can only be run once cannot be verified by re-running it.
>

>
> **The mitigation was a habit, and a habit is not a check.**
>
> Every backgrounded `codex exec` call tonight — 16 of 16 — hit a bug that silently
> withholds the prompt: Codex prints "Reading additional input from stdin…", waits, and
> **exits 0 having done nothing**. All 16 ran anyway and produced 132 KB–4 MB each, for one
> reason: every call carried `< /dev/null`, so the read hit EOF immediately and Codex fell
> through to the prompt already on argv. Nothing was retracted. But that outcome was bought
> by a typing habit, not by anything that would have failed had the habit lapsed.
>
> Generalise it past Codex: **any step whose success you infer rather than verify is a
> habit.** A gate with no `sys.exit(1)`; a numerical witness that SKIPs for a missing
> baseline and is counted as a pass; a build whose log stayed empty because the wrapper
> unbuffered only the text layer and not the `BufferedWriter` beneath it — all the same

## Who the reader is, and therefore what "good" means

Mahmood, 2026-08-24: *"the idea is that you write the manuscript and then we check and
rewrite it. this is important as a lot of people doing are med students so unfamiliar."*

The generator's output is a **first draft for a novice to correct**, not a publication. That
reconciles the two targets this session spent the day arguing between: manuscript-shaped is
right (a student needs to see what they are building toward), with the honesty of the
evidence-report form, plus explicit markers of what needs a human.

**The acceptance test is no longer "does this read like a published review."** It is:

> Can a medical student who does not know this field turn this into a good paper
> **without being misled by it**?

Put to three blind reviewers under that brief, the current sotagliflozin draft scored
**3 of 3 DANGEROUS** — and not for style. For two internal contradictions a novice resolves
by picking whichever half is easier to write up. One reviewer named the mechanism exactly:
*"A novice will report LOW because it is a concrete answer that fits their template."*

Four properties follow, and they outrank readability:

1. **Every claim traceable**, so a student can check rather than trust.
2. **Absences visible and actionable**, phrased as work to do — "follow-up duration was not
   extracted; find it in the trial report and add it here" — never smoothed over. A
   confident sentence over missing data is the worst possible output for this audience,
   because it survives their edit.
3. **Anything derived, marked as derived.** Tonight's "imprecision — the intervals remain
   wide" was ours, not the data's; three reviewers disputed it and a student would have
   kept it.
4. **Where only a human can judge — rationale, synthesis, interpretation — say so and leave
   the space**, rather than filling it plausibly.

This brief found in one pass two defects that six rounds of "does it read like a review"
never surfaced. Use it.

## NEVER WRITE A REGEX THROUGH A HEREDOC — USE THE EDITOR

Not "remember to escape". **Remove the opportunity.**

> **This is not a style preference and must not be relaxed into one.** It is a mechanical
> constraint on which tool may touch which content. If you find yourself about to write a
> backslash inside a heredoc, the answer is not "be careful" -- it is to use the Edit tool
> instead. The rule survived being written into the code twice, into a regression test once,
> and into this handover -- and was violated four times the same day, INCLUDING inside the
> paragraph documenting it, where `backslash-b` and `backslash-1` arrived as 0x08 and 0x01
> and the pre-commit lint refused the commit. A rule that asks for attention fails under
> load; a rule that removes the opportunity does not.

Written through a shell heredoc, `backslash-b` arrives in the source as a literal BACKSPACE (0x08)
and `backslash-1` as SOH (0x01). The regex then matches nothing and the function returns its input
unchanged, while `inspect.getsource` prints code that reads correctly. It is invisible
except to `cat -A`, which renders them `^H` and `^A`.

**This happened FOUR times on 2026-08-24** — after the rule was written into
`paper_projector.py` twice and into `test_instrument_regressions_2026_08_24.py` as case 4.
`.githooks/pre-push` runs `lint_control_chars.py` for exactly this class, and that hook's own
comment records it recurring eight times in one night against an author who had read the
rule and committed it.

That is the strongest evidence available that **care-based rules do not hold**. A rule that
asks for more attention fails under load; a rule that removes the opportunity does not. Use
the Edit tool for anything containing a backslash.

## FIXING THE INSTANCES RATHER THAN THE CLASS — and it happens to hand-edits too

We named this rule against a *generator* two days ago: a fix applied where the scan reported
the defect, rather than everywhere the property has to hold. On 2026-08-25 it arrived again
through an entirely manual edit, which is why it is worth its own section.

The ARNI amendment targeted three named sites — `blocks[90].caption`, `blocks[101].text`,
`blocks[68].rows` — because those were the three the scan printed. The amendment applied
cleanly, the source verified clean (`dict repr gone: True`), the page rebuilt, and the
delivered page **still carried the dict**. The same text was also in `blocks[235].text` and
`blocks[252].text`. Two more copies, forty blocks further down, that nothing had asked about.

**A scan reports where it looked. It does not report a population.** The three indices were
an artefact of what the scan surfaced first, and I treated them as the extent of the defect
because they came from a measurement and therefore felt measured. They were not: the number
three was reviewer reach, exactly like the panel's "five pages" that turned out to be fifteen.

The corrected amendment sweeps every block and every string field for the property, and does
not accept a list of sites from anywhere. **Where the fix is a hand-edit rather than a code
change, the sweep matters more, not less** — a generator at least applies its logic uniformly
by construction, whereas a hand-edit applies exactly where the hand went.

The operational rule, and it now has three independent confirmations in one week:

> Never take the sites a scan named as the population. Re-derive the population from the
> property, sweep it, and print the count you swept — including for manual edits, and
> including when the scan that named the sites was one you wrote and trust.

Related and worth reading together: the projector instance in this file, the panel's
"five named, fifteen true", and the four over-flags of 2026-08-25 where the same instinct
ran in the *opposite* direction and manufactured defect classes that did not exist.

## THE PANEL IS A HYPOTHESIS GENERATOR, NOT AN ORACLE

**26 of 38 of its complaints did not survive adjudication.**

Every student MISLEADING verdict from the corpus panel quotes a sentence the reader believed
was misleading. Those 38 quoted claims were each put back to a second family, against the
delivered page **and** the canonical object, and asked to return CONFIRMED, REFUTED or
UNVERIFIABLE with a severity.

| | |
|---|---|
| CONFIRMED | **11** (10 of them HIGH severity) |
| REFUTED | **26** |
| UNVERIFIABLE | 1 |

Two-thirds of the panel's worst pages were a reader disagreeing with a convention, or
misreading a page that was right. The panel's value is that it looks where our gates
structurally cannot — it found the I² contradiction that no field-level check could see,
because that defect lives between two sentences rather than between a sentence and a field.
Its value is *not* that its verdicts are correct.

**This nearly cost a true sentence.** The panel-seeded hunt flagged 16 pages for an abstract
stating a certainty level over a body reading "0 pooled outcome(s) were rated". Every
instance that week had been a confident abstract over a body that knew better, so the obvious
move was to suppress the abstract. The object said otherwise: AGYW_HIV_PREP holds
`pooled {point 0.703, CI 0.566 to 0.873, RR}` with k=2 and GRADE certainty LOW. A real pool,
really rated. **The abstract was right and the body was wrong** — the GRADE section counted on
a `rated` flag that 13 of 30 objects never set, and then *invented* a reason for its own
miscount, attributing "their pool is declined or withdrawn" to outcomes whose pools were
neither.

The operating rule:

> A panel verdict is a CLAIM. Adjudicate it against the object before acting on it, and read
> the object before touching the prose. The direction of the defect is not knowable from the
> shape of the complaint.

And the same discipline applies to the panel's *aggregate* claims about individual pages.
Replication on ARNI — 3 published anchors, 2 personas, 2 families, both orderings, 24 jobs —
inverted its round-1 result: DESK-REJECT 10 of 12, and 4 of 4 against the anchor it had
"beaten". Eleven of twelve editor cells were position-dependent. **Any single page verdict is
one draw with roughly a 1-in-6 chance of flipping on family alone.** The aggregate over 149
pages is the measurement; a per-page verdict is an anecdote. Cross-family agreement on
re-read pages is 83% (34 of 41), with disagreements symmetric and neither family lenient, so
the aggregates stand.

## WHEN A COUNT CHANGES AFTER A FIX, THE FIRST HYPOTHESIS IS THAT THE INSTRUMENT MOVED

Sits beside the scan rule at the top of this file, and is its mirror image. That rule is
about a list of sites being smaller than the population. This one is about the *number*
moving for reasons that have nothing to do with the corpus.

**Eight over-flags in one day, 2026-08-25, every one mine, every one caught by a control
rather than by reading:**

| Instrument | Said | True | What actually moved |
|---|---|---|---|
| I² contradiction, v1 | 38 | 15 | matched any I² against any "not pooled" anywhere on the page |
| I² contradiction, v2 | 25 | 15 | the estimate pattern omitted "mean difference" and required the CI to open on a digit, not a minus |
| short-page rendering | 97 | 0 | counted any populated result block as a pool; k=1 single-trial pages are correct |
| object lookup | 0 | n/a | keyed on the wrong path, found no objects for all 149 pages, printed "0 failures" from it |
| search-claim, v1 | 5 | 3 | matched prose against prose; two pages named PubMed and had really run it |
| GRADE certainty | 16 | 13 | conflated "certainty was rated with GRADE" (a method) with "certainty was low" (a result) |
| Pages deployment | "15-day outage" | none | `/pages/builds` only reports LEGACY builds; this repo is `build_type: workflow` |
| search-claim, v2 | 1 | 0 | could not tell a claim from a disclaimer, and flagged a page for the sentence that FIXED it |
| `CLASS_TOPICS` exclusion | 130 swept | 149 | **the inversion**: a guard written to prevent over-flagging produced UNDER-checking. The substring `statin` excluded PITAVASTATIN and ROSUVASTATIN — two specific drugs — from a defect sweep because their names end in the class they belong to. ARNI, SGLT2_HF and ACS_ANTIPLATELET went unchecked for the same reason, and ACS_ANTIPLATELET turned out to be a MISMATCH. |
| commit message | "all 19 CLEAN" | 17 CLEAN, 2 MISMATCH | a conclusion written from 17 visible lines of a 19-line run **while the run was still going** — and one of the two mismatches was a page named in the same sentence. The only one not caught by a control, because commit prose has none. Corrected in a follow-up commit rather than amended away. |

Two of those deserve singling out because they are the failure modes that cost most:

**A control anchored to the live worktree retires itself the moment its defect is fixed.**
Twice now. The positive control stops detecting anything, the instrument refuses to print,
and the natural reading is "the instrument broke" rather than "the fix worked". Anchor every
positive control to the page **as committed at a named pre-fix SHA** — a page in git history
cannot be repaired out from under its own control.

**An instrument that cannot tell a claim from a disclaimer punishes the pages that got it
right.** After the projector was fixed to name unsearched sources honestly, the audit flagged
the page for saying "no search was executed against anzctr" — accusing it on the strength of
the very sentence that fixed it. That is the worst direction an instrument can fail in,
because it reverses the incentive.

The rule, operationally:

> A count that moves after a fix is evidence about the instrument until proved otherwise.
> Re-run the positive control first. If the control passes and the count still moved, the
> corpus moved. If the control fails, nothing has been learned and no number may be quoted.

And the reason this is a good day rather than a bad one: eight caught cost an afternoon.
**Eight uncaught would have produced eight defect classes that do not exist**, each sending a
fix to the wrong layer, each burning review time that real defects needed. Over-flagging is
not the safe direction.

## THE POOLABILITY GATE IS PROTECTING YOU FROM A DEFECT NOBODY KNOWS IT CATCHES

**Read this before relaxing any poolability rule.**

A sweep of 139 delivered pages on 2026-08-25 found **29 pages carrying a trial that does not
study the page's subject** — 42 trial records: 18 where the drug is ABSENT from the trial, 23
where the page's drug is the **COMPARATOR** arm, 1 where it is **BACKGROUND** therapy given
to everyone.

**None of the 42 reached a published pooled estimate.** All 42 sit on pages that publish no
pool at all. So the damage is display-only: a reader is shown trials that do not belong, but
no number is wrong, and no direction flip needs correcting.

**That is luck, not design.** The poolability rules reject on endpoint identity and contrast
identity. A trial set containing a wrong drug rarely satisfies endpoint identity — so the
poolability gate has been silently absorbing the matcher's errors, *downstream of them, and
without looking for them*. It fires for an unrelated reason and happens to catch this class
on the way past.

Two consequences, and the second is the one that will matter in six months:

1. **The matcher defect was invisible** precisely because its consequences were absorbed. No
   page ever showed a wrong pooled number, so nothing surfaced.
2. **Relaxing a poolability rule removes a safeguard nobody knows they have.** Any future
   change that loosens endpoint or contrast identity — to include more trials, to raise k, to
   let a near-miss outcome through — will simultaneously open the path from a mismatched
   trial to a published estimate. The protection is not where anyone would look for it, and
   it is not written down anywhere except here.

**Before loosening poolability, run the trial-identity sweep and require it clean.** The two
changes are coupled even though nothing in the code says so.

The matcher itself is `find_ncts` in `scripts/add_topic_autodiscover.py`; the identity rule is
now in `ssot/trial_identity.py` with named controls. **It cannot be re-run on this machine** —
it reads an AACT snapshot, `AACT_DIR` is unset and `~/AACT` does not exist. So the rule ships
validated on nine hand-checked cases, not on the corpus, and that limit is stated in the test
rather than papered over.

## PROSE IS THE ONE ARTEFACT WITH NO VERIFICATION

Every count on a page is traceable to a field. Every gate has a control. **Commit messages
and reports have neither**, and they are where claims are actually made — to Mahmood, and to
whoever reads this next.

On 2026-08-25 the only failure of the day that no control caught was a sentence: a commit
message asserting "all 19 CLEAN" written from 17 visible lines of a 19-line job **that was
still running**. Two of the remaining pages were MISMATCH, and one of them, ACS_ANTIPLATELET,
was named in the same sentence that got it wrong. Eight instrument over-flags were caught
that day because instruments have controls. The ninth shipped because prose does not.

**We are asking the corpus to be traceable while writing untraceable prose about it.**

Two rules, both cheap:

1. **Never write a summary claim from a job that is still running.** If a count is quoted, it
   comes from a *completed* artefact, read *after* completion. A partial run's output is not
   a smaller version of the answer — it is a different answer, and usually a flattering one,
   because the interesting cases are not evenly distributed through a queue.

2. **Any commit message or report asserting a count states where the count came from** — the
   artefact and the run — so a reader can check it the way a reader checks a page. Not
   "29 pages mismatched" but "29 of 139, `outputs/trial_identity_sweep_2026_08_25.jsonl`,
   111 records at the time of reading". A count without a source is exactly the kind of
   sentence this project spends its time removing from manuscripts.

The asymmetry is the point: a wrong number on a page gets caught by a gate, and a wrong
number in a commit message becomes what everyone believes.

## THE NEAR-MISS: A GREEN SUITE THAT WOULD HAVE DELETED HEAD-TO-HEAD TRIALS FOREVER

The worked example for *a reference standard must be audited before it can certify*, written
out in full because **the counterfactual is the lesson, not the fix.**

**What I wrote.** A trial-identity rule to replace the matcher's substring test. Its
comparator clause read, in effect: *the drug must appear in an arm whose registered type is
`EXPERIMENTAL`; if it appears only in a comparator arm, the trial does not study it.* That
sounds right. It is how a person describes a drug trial. It passed every case I had.

**What I was about to validate it against.** The trial-identity sweep's own `role` labels —
`EXPERIMENTAL`, `COMPARATOR`, `BACKGROUND`, `ABSENT` — assigned by a model reading trial
titles. 42 records of ready-made ground truth.

**What the registry actually says** for NCT02829957 (RAMBLE), which the sweep had labelled
`role=COMPARATOR` for apixaban:

```
ACTIVE_COMPARATOR   Rivaroxaban
ACTIVE_COMPARATOR   Apixaban
```

**Neither arm is `EXPERIMENTAL`.** It is a head-to-head trial, and apixaban is the
*hypothesised better* drug — the sweep's label is simply wrong. It had reached the right
verdict (RAMBLE does not belong on an apixaban VTE page) for the wrong reason: the trial
measures **menstrual blood loss**, an outcome mismatch, not an arm-role one.

**The counterfactual, which is the whole point.** Had I validated the rule against those
labels:

- the rule would have **agreed** with the label on RAMBLE — both say "not studied" — so the
  case would have **passed**;
- the test suite would have been **green**;
- the rule would have shipped requiring an `EXPERIMENTAL` arm;
- and it would have **silently rejected every head-to-head trial in the corpus**, because
  active-controlled trials frequently register both arms as `ACTIVE_COMPARATOR` with no
  experimental label at all.

Head-to-head is *the* design for drug comparison. The rule would have excluded precisely the
evidence that matters most for the question this project exists to answer — and nobody would
have looked, because the suite was green and the rule sounded right. A dropped trial leaves
**no trace on a page**: no refusal, no gap, no odd sentence. Unlike a wrong trial, which at
least looks strange to a reader, a missing one is invisible by construction.

**What caught it** was not review and not a test. It was refusing to accept ready-made ground
truth and going to the registration instead — one API call, in the hour after writing the
rule that *a reference standard must be audited before it can certify*.

Two smaller things fell out of the same read, both of which would also have shipped green:

- **TWILIGHT (NCT02270242)** lists ticagrelor in *both* arms; the randomised contrast is
  aspirin against placebo. The tell for background therapy is not an arm *type* — it is that
  the drug appears in **every** arm. A type-based rule cannot see this at all.
- The API returns `"Drug: Cefepime/VNRX-5133"`. The combination splitter compared
  `"drug: cefepime"` against `"cefepime"`, never matched, and therefore **passed** a bare
  `cefepime` pattern against the taniborbactam combination — the exact case the test exists
  to catch, defeated by a five-character type prefix.

Three defects in one rule, all found by one honest reference standard, none findable by
reading the code.

## VERIFY A COMMIT BY ITS CONTENT, NOT BY THE ABSENCE OF AN ERROR

A standing habit rather than a rule, because it has now bitten in several disguises.

**2026-08-25.** A commit was composed, the hook passed, and nothing landed:

```
git add scripts/x.py outputs/y.json && STAGING_WIDE=1 git commit -q -F - <<'EOF' ...
```

`outputs/y.json` was gitignored, so `git add` returned **non-zero**, the `&&` short-circuited,
and the commit never ran. What was then observed:

- `git log --oneline -1` → the **previous** commit, which looks like a commit
- `git push` → **"Everything up-to-date"**, which reads like success

Both outputs are consistent with "it worked". Neither is evidence that it did.

**This is the pipe-exit-code family**, and the members keep arriving in new clothes:

| disguise | what was read as success |
|---|---|
| `cmd \| tail` then `$?` | `tail`'s exit code, always 0 |
| `git add X && git commit` | a short-circuited chain, no error printed |
| `git push` on an unchanged HEAD | "Everything up-to-date" |
| a background job that dies early | an empty log, read as "quiet" |

The habit:

> After any commit, **verify it exists by its content** — `git log -1 --stat`, or grep the
> message for a string you know you wrote. After any push, verify the **remote ref moved**.
> "No error appeared" is not a result; it is the absence of one.

The same shape as *a fetch failure is not an absence* and *unknown is not no*: **silence is
not information**, and every one of these bugs works by making silence look like a
confirmation.

## A ZERO FROM A MEASUREMENT WHOSE PRECONDITION IS ABSENT IS THE MOST DANGEROUS NUMBER WE PRODUCE

**It reads as evidence of absence and it is absence of evidence — and it always lands against
whichever side the measurement was built to doubt.**

2026-08-25, caught by a guard rather than by reading. A data-recovery measurement was widened
to accept more table captions, and immediately reported, for PMC13491898:

```
outcome rows 11 | NCT ids named 15
rows RESOLVED to a trial 0 of 11
RECOVERED 0%
```

Read straight, that says *ClinicalTrials.gov holds none of this review's data.* It says
nothing of the kind. That table's row labels are **OS, PFS, ORR, DCR, TRAE** — one row per
**outcome**, with pooled numbers. There are no trials in that column to resolve. The
precondition for the measurement — a per-trial table — was absent, and the instrument
reported on it anyway.

**Why this asymmetry matters.** The measurement existed to test whether open sources are
sufficient. A spurious 0% would have been evidence *for* the position the measurement was
built to doubt, which is the direction least likely to be questioned by the person running
it. A spurious 100% would have been interrogated immediately.

Operationally:

- **State the precondition, then test it, then measure.** "Does this review print per-trial
  numbers?" is a different question from "can those numbers be recovered?", and answering the
  second without the first produces a number about neither.
- **Report `NO PRECONDITION` as its own state**, never folded into the failure count. In this
  instrument a review with no per-trial table is not 0% recovered; it is not measurable, and
  those are different rows in the output.
- The same shape as *unknown is not no*, *a fetch failure is not an absence*, and *a
  numerical witness SKIP is not a pass* — but sharper, because here the missing precondition
  **manufactures a plausible number** rather than an obvious blank.

The guard now requires a row label to carry a trial identity — an NCT, a year, or a name
longer than an endpoint acronym — before the row is treated as a trial at all.

## A JUDGE IS A VARIABLE. RECORD IT, AND DO NOT CALL ONE MODEL'S PRIOR AN ADJUDICATION.

From the site lane, measured on the same class of finding:

| judge | refutes |
|---|---|
| OpenAI | 36% |
| Google | 63% |
| Anthropic | 80% |

**A 2.2× spread.** Their framing is the one to keep: *the third model isn't breaking a tie,
it's voting with a strong undisclosed prior.*

**This reframes a number reported here.** The panel's worst 38 claims were adjudicated to
11 CONFIRMED / 26 REFUTED / 1 UNVERIFIABLE — a **68% attrition** that was used to conclude
"the panel is a hypothesis generator, not an oracle". Every one of those verdicts came from
**one judge, `codex exec`, OpenAI** — and the ledger did not say so until it was relabelled
retroactively.

The conclusion survives; **the size does not.** 68% attrition from the *most lenient* of the
three judges is a different fact from 68% from the harshest, and until a second judge runs we
do not know which way it moves. Queued: re-adjudicate a sample with Google and Anthropic when
the agy quota resets.

Two rules:

- **Every adjudicated verdict records its judge.** A verdict without one is missing its
  largest single variable, and the omission is invisible in the ledger rather than obvious.
- **Use finding counts to RANK, adjudicate individual findings.** Mean pairwise sentence
  overlap between families is **7.6%** with severity agreement 40–50% — families agree a page
  is bad and disagree almost entirely about *why*. A count is the union of largely disjoint
  opinions, not a measurement of one thing, and should never be treated as one.

## A PER-VENDOR FAILURE RATE OF 0% VERSUS 100% IS A HARNESS SIGNAL, NOT A MODEL DIFFERENCE

**Real model differences are graded.** A clean split by vendor is infrastructure.

Confirmed on this lane's own data before the rule arrived. The four-family panel:

| family | verdicts | no output |
|---|---|---|
| openai | 50 | **0** |
| google | 50 | **0** |
| anthropic | 12 | **38** |
| open_weights | 11 | **39** |

Two vendors at zero failures, two at ~78%. That is not four models disagreeing about a task —
it is two pools behind one wall. The cause was a **shared individual quota** that neither the
weekly nor the five-hour meter displayed, and the diagnostic identifies it in one glance
without needing to find the error message.

The general form: **before attributing a split to capability, check whether it partitions
cleanly by vendor, seat, host, or path.** A graded difference invites a model explanation; a
step function does not.
