## Three things I got wrong today, in my own words

**I measured where I looked and reported it as the population.** I checked whether GRADE's
rationales cited the risk-of-bias assessors and reported 11 of 11 independent. That was
true of the field I read — `grade.domains.risk_of_bias.basis_in_sources`, the prose — and
silent on the sibling key one step away, `grade.risk_of_bias.now_supported_by`, which
carries the number: "2 of 2 results are HIGH overall, all of them on domain 5." The prose
argues independently because a later pass rewrote it; the rating it justifies was set by
reading one assessor and was never revisited. Both halves true, and the finding I reported
was the half that flattered. This was the dominant shape of my errors all day, and it
recurred inside checks written to catch it.

**A code path that had never run.** My native-schema reader passed arni's overall judgement
through as a dict where the domains branch six lines above correctly unwraps it. It crashed
the first time anything invoked it — arni was not among the 25 pages rebuilt tonight, so
nothing had. This is the fourth gate category in its sharpest form: not merely unexercised
but *broken* and unexercised, which is indistinguishable from correct until something runs
it. An audit that enumerates gates and finds this one will call it healthy.

**A derived figure does not look like a judgement until you ask where the number came
from.** I searched twice for places that publish one assessor's judgements as the review's
finding, fixed the panel and the manuscript, and called the class closed. The third
instance rendered a *count derived from* those judgements — "the domain carrying that
judgement is D5 on 3 result(s)" — and my search could not see it, because I had searched
for renderers of judgements rather than for consumers of them. It took a reviewer to find.
The search-design lesson: enumerate what READS a field, not what DISPLAYS it.
