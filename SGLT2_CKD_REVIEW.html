Pooled result

Estimate withdrawn. The three trials do not share one endpoint definition, and each registry record says so in its own words. All three composites count kidney-function decline, but at three different thresholds: CREDENCE counts a doubling of serum creatinine, DAPA-CKD counts a sustained decline of at least 50 per cent in eGFR, and EMPA-KIDNEY counts a sustained decline of at least 40 per cent in eGFR. Those are three different events, and the decline component is the largest contributor to each composite: a 40 per cent threshold admits strictly more patients than a 50 per cent threshold, which admits strictly more than a doubling of creatinine. The end-stage component differs too -- CREDENCE and DAPA-CKD floor it at an eGFR below 15, EMPA-KIDNEY at an eGFR below 10. Separately and sufficient on its own: every per-trial value this review held was an odds ratio derived by US from 2x2 counts, while all three trials analyse time to first occurrence -- DAPA-CKD's registry record states that 'the proportional hazards Cox regression model takes into account the time to the event'. The index card published that derived odds ratio under the label HR.

What is confirmed, stated as prominently as what is wrong. The component types agree across all three trials: every one counts kidney-function decline, end-stage kidney disease, renal death and cardiovascular death, and every one counts the first of them to occur. EMPA-KIDNEY's registry title names only 'kidney disease progression or cardiovascular death' and its description places renal death and the eGFR thresholds inside 'kidney disease progression' -- so reading the title alone would have made it look like a trial counting no renal death at all, which would have argued for splitting it from the other two for the wrong reason. The full description is quoted on every trial for exactly that reason. These three trials are close; they are not the same, and the difference is a threshold rather than a missing component. And the three results agree: crude risk ratios of 0.72, 0.63 and 0.77, all in the same direction. Agreement of results is not evidence of a common estimand. What is averaged is decided by what was counted, not by how the counts came out, and this withdrawal cannot be undone by observing that the numbers are close. The replacement question is identified and deliberately not taken: end-stage kidney disease as dialysis or transplant, and cardiovascular death, are each defined identically across all three and each is reported separately by all three. Taking it needs the trials' own reported hazard ratios for those components, from the registry results sections, and -- as recorded on ABLATION_AF -- a replacement question must be chosen before its answer is known. It is parked as a decision, not left as an oversight.
