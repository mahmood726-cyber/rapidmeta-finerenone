**DOCUMENT A**

ARE THE METHODS SOUND AS REPORTED?
Yes, the methods are exceptionally sound and rigorously justified. The document makes pooling decisions based on exact endpoint definitions rather than assumed similarities, refusing to pool mismatched composites: "The four trials do not share one endpoint. DAPA-HF and DELIVER count cardiovascular death, hospitalisation for heart failure, or an urgent heart-failure visit; emperor-Reduced and EMPEROR-Preserved count cardiovascular death or hospitalisation for heart failure only. Two trials count an event class the other two do not, so this pool combined quantities that are not the same quantity." It justifies excluding a trial from a pool because it measured a different statistical quantity: "Its registry secondaries are RECURRENT-EVENT composites (HR 0.77), which is a different quantity again... A k=3 pool we can fully vouch for beats A k=4 with one input we cannot". 

The certainty ratings and handling of heterogeneity are brutally honest. It reports full heterogeneity statistics (I-squared, tau-squared, Q, df) but refuses to blindly trust a low I-squared, noting "A LOW i-squared does not show the trials measure the same thing... coherence is established by reading the definitions". It downgrades for imprecision despite tight confidence intervals because it recognizes the mathematical limitations of a small pool: "at k = 3 tau-squared is estimated from almost no information and a random-effects interval is optimistic even when it looks tight. Rated down for a small contributing set rather than reporting the number as if it were robust." It correctly handles publication bias by stating: "publication bias is undetected rather than ABSENT. Not rated down, and not reported as clean: an unreachable state is not a passing one." At no point is a number presented with more confidence than its derivation supports.

**DOCUMENT B**

ARE THE METHODS SOUND AS REPORTED?
No, the methods are opaque and the reporting conceals uncertainty. The document provides no justification for its pooling decisions beyond stating, "Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs were pooled across trials". There is no verification that the three included trials defined their composite endpoints (like "major adverse cardiovascular events" or "progression of renal disease") in the exact same way. 

The document completely ignores certainty ratings. There is no risk of bias assessment, no GRADE rating, and no report of statistical heterogeneity (such as I-squared or tau-squared) for the overall pools. Consequently, the document presents its findings with a level of absolute certainty that a meta-analysis of only three trials cannot support. It states as definitive fact that "SGLT2i reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 11%" and concludes they have "robust benefits," entirely hiding any methodological or statistical uncertainty from the reader.

**WHICH IS BETTER**

Document A is decisively better. 

What separates them is the honest representation of uncertainty and strict discipline regarding what can actually be pooled. Document A interrogates the underlying definitions of the trials ("Verified word for word against each trial's registry entry") and refuses to combine "quantities that are not the same quantity." It explicitly acknowledges its own mathematical limitations, refusing to pretend a 3-trial random-effects model is perfectly precise. Document B does the exact opposite: it silently assumes the trials are similar enough to pool, runs the math, and presents the resulting point estimates as unassailable facts ("robust benefits") while completely ignoring risk of bias, heterogeneity, and the inherent imprecision of a small meta-analysis.
