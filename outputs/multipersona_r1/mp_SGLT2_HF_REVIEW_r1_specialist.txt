DOCUMENT A

Document A is **misleading by omission** and its clinical reasoning is **factually incorrect**. 

First, it refuses to pool all four heart failure trials for the primary endpoint, stating: *"Two trials count an event class the other two do not, so this pool combined quantities that are not the same quantity."* This is clinically false. An "urgent heart-failure visit" requiring intravenous therapy is functionally and clinically synonymous with an HF hospitalisation; they represent the exact same worsening heart failure pathophysiology. Refusing to pool them is bureaucratic pedantry that obscures the true class effect.

Second, it excludes the landmark DELIVER trial from the two-component composite pool (CV death or HF hospitalisation), stating: *"Its harmonised value exists in the publication, not the registration. A k=3 pool we can fully vouch for beats A k=4 with one input we cannot"*. This is a catastrophic omission. Throwing out a pivotal 6,263-patient trial simply because its first-event two-component outcome is reported in the peer-reviewed publication rather than the registry artificially shrinks the evidence base and grossly misleads a reader about the completeness and precision of the data.

DOCUMENT B

Document B is **overstated relative to the evidence** and **misleading by omission**. 

It concludes that SGLT2 inhibitors have *"robust benefits on reducing hospitalisation for heart failure and progression of renal disease regardless of existing atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or a history of heart failure."* Claiming a robust benefit for patients with a "history of heart failure" based on this dataset is a massive overstatement. The three included trials (enrolling 34,322 patients) are cardiovascular outcome trials in type 2 diabetes, not dedicated heart failure trials. The subgroup with a "history of heart failure" comprised only about 10–15% of the population, and their heart failure was poorly phenotyped—relying entirely on unadjudicated investigator reports without mandatory ejection fraction criteria or background guideline-directed medical therapy. 

What is missing that changes the interpretation is that these trials primarily demonstrated the *prevention* of incident heart failure in a diabetic population, not the *treatment* of established heart failure. A reader would wrongly conclude from this document that these three trials constitute definitive proof for treating established heart failure, entirely omitting the need for the actual dedicated heart failure trials (like DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced) that were required to prove it.

WHICH IS BETTER, A or B

Document B is **decisively better**. 

What separates them is that Document B draws slightly overenthusiastic subgroup conclusions from a dataset it comprehensively analyzed, whereas Document A intentionally destroys its own analysis out of administrative pedantry. Document B provides a clinically useful meta-analysis of the diabetes outcome trials as they stood. Document A, by insisting that *"A k=3 pool we can fully vouch for beats A k=4 with one input we cannot"* and that an urgent HF visit makes a composite *"not the same quantity,"* actively chooses to ignore peer-reviewed data and valid clinical homogeneity. Document A fails the fundamental purpose of a systematic review by willfully omitting available, pivotal evidence.
