WHAT EACH OF THE THREE COSTS

FOLLOW-UP DURATION -- the cheapest, and it is not close.
   Held on 0 of 50 topics. It is a single field on the registration record
   (`time frame` on the primary outcome, and the study start/completion dates),
   and every one of these 155 registrations has already been fetched once by this
   pipeline -- `registration_read_utc` is stamped on them. So this is a re-fetch
   of an endpoint we already call, plus a field to store. Fully automatable, no
   reading, no judgement. One pass over 155 registrations.

ARM-LEVEL EVENT COUNTS -- splits sharply in two, and the split is the estimate.
   For the 124 trials whose object already shows a results posting, the counts are
   structured data on an endpoint we call today: automatable.
   For the 33 where no posting is recorded, the numbers exist only in the
   published report, which means a person opens a paper, finds the outcome table,
   and transcribes two numerators and two denominators per outcome. That is the
   irreducible human cost, and it is the bulk of bucket 2.
   NOTE: 'not recorded' is not 'not posted'. This corpus never asked the question,
   so the true automatable share is AT LEAST 124 and may be much higher. Probing
   the results endpoint for all 155 is itself a one-pass automated job, and it
   should be done BEFORE anyone commits to the manual figure -- costing the manual
   work against an unknown denominator is how this session already produced one
   badly wrong estimate.

HARMS -- automatable to fetch, NOT automatable to scope.
   A registry results posting carries the full adverse-event tables, so retrieval
   is the same job as the counts above. What cannot be automated is DECIDING WHICH
   HARMS THIS REVIEW REPORTS: a posting lists every event at every grade, and
   choosing the ones a reader needs -- serious adverse events, discontinuations,
   the drug-class harms a clinician asks about -- is a judgement about relevance.
   That makes harms a bucket-2 fetch with a bucket-3 decision attached, and it is
   the only one of the three that cannot be finished without an author.

SEQUENCING THAT FALLS OUT OF THIS
   1. Probe the results endpoint for all 155 reachable registrations. One automated
      pass. It converts the 33 unknowns into a real number and costs almost
      nothing. Nothing else should be committed to before it returns.
   2. Take follow-up duration in the same pass. It is free once the record is open.
   3. Ingest arm counts for every trial with a posting. Automated.
   4. Then, and only then, count what is left for manual extraction and price it.
   5. Harms last, because it needs a rule about what to report before it needs data.
