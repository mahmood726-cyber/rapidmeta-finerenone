### Overall Verdict

Yes, there is a severe, 100% systematic problem with the dashboard's data integrity. 

**The pattern:** In every single row, the arm counts and the summary effect measure point in **mathematically opposite directions relative to the null (1.0).** 

*   When the displayed effect shows a benefit $> 1$ (Rows 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8), the raw counts show fewer positive events in the treatment group (yielding an implied effect $< 1$).
*   When the displayed effect shows a benefit $< 1$ (Row 2, negative outcome), the raw counts show more negative events in the treatment group (yielding an implied effect $> 1$).
*   When the raw counts show a benefit $> 1$ (Row 6, positive outcome with implied RR 16.0), the displayed effect shows a value $< 1$ (RR 0.28).

**Conclusion:** The dashboard is systematically divorcing the summary statistics from the raw data. For seven of the eight trials, the dashboard is rendering the `Comparator` counts under the `Treatment` header (and vice versa) while keeping the effect estimate correct. In the single case where the counts are assigned to the correct arms (Lesinurad), the dashboard inverted the effect estimate instead. As a result, the displayed counts mathematically contradict the displayed effect size 100% of the time.
