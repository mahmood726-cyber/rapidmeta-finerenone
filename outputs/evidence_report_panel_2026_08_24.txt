**2. Is this HONEST?**
Yes, brutally so. It bends over backwards to avoid the academic spin that plagues cardiology literature.

**4. Quote anything that is confusing, ambiguous or badly worded, and say what is wrong with it.**
- *"Total occurrences of cardiovascular death, hospitalization for heart failure and urgent heart failure visit, as a hazard ratio"*
  **Diagnosis:** Epidemiologically clumsy. "Total occurrences" implies a raw count or a cumulative incidence, but a hazard ratio is a relative measure of instantaneous risk over time. If they ran a recurrent event analysis, they should call it a hazard ratio for recurrent events. You do not express "total occurrences" as a hazard ratio. 
- *"...and cardiovascular risk"* (in the SCORED population description).
  **Diagnosis:** Vague and undefined. Everyone has some baseline cardiovascular risk. Does this mean established atherosclerotic disease? Multiple risk factors? A specific risk score threshold? It tells me nothing precise about the inclusion criteria.
- *"How each estimate was obtained: the printed hazard ratio, stored on the log scale with the standard error its printed interval implies."*
  **Diagnosis:** This is a metadata note for a statistician's codebook, not clinical reporting. It just means "we extracted the published HRs and CIs." It is jarring, overly technical, and irrelevant to a clinical reader.

**5. Is anything MISSING that you would need for this document to do its job?**
Yes. Even if its job is purely to report evidence without interpretation, an "evidence report" is structurally incomplete without:
- **Absolute event rates:** Event counts and denominators per arm. Reporting only relative effects (HRs) is reporting a derivative, not the actual evidence of what happened.
- **Follow-up duration:** A hazard ratio is meaningless without knowing the timeframe. Did this hazard reduction accumulate over 3 months or 5 years?
- **Safety data:** Reporting efficacy evidence without reporting the corresponding harm evidence is an unacceptable truncation of the clinical picture, even if you refuse to interpret the balance between them.
