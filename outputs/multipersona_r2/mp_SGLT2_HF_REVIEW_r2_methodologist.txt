**DOCUMENT A**

ARE THE METHODS SOUND AS REPORTED? No. The methods are functionally absent.
Document A does not justify its pooling decisions at all; it merely asserts that "Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs were pooled across trials". There is zero discussion of whether the endpoints in these trials were defined consistently or whether the populations were sufficiently similar to justify combining them. 
It hides heterogeneity completely. There is no mention of I-squared, tau-squared, any statistical test for variance, or any formal certainty rating applied to the evidence. 
Uncertainty is entirely hidden, and the numbers are presented with absolute, unearned confidence. It states as fact that "SGLT2i reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 11% (HR 0.89 [95% CI 0.83-0.96], p=0.0014)". The document claims robust findings without ever disclosing the meta-analytic model used, let alone whether the underlying trials were actually measuring the same quantities.

**DOCUMENT B**

ARE THE METHODS SOUND AS REPORTED? Yes, exceptionally so.
Document B meticulously justifies its pooling decisions based on exact endpoint definitions verified at the registry level. It explicitly refuses to pool mismatched endpoints, stating: "These 4 trials are not pooled, and the reason is stated rather than the outcome being quietly omitted: The four trials do not share one endpoint... Two trials count an event class the other two do not, so this pool combined quantities that are not the same quantity." Where it does pool, it requires that "The contributing results share one endpoint definition, verified by reading each trial's registered text rather than by comparing names."
It handles heterogeneity and certainty ratings honestly. It reports full heterogeneity statistics but correctly observes that "A LOW i-squared does not show the trials measure the same thing... coherence is established by reading the definitions". It rates certainty as "LOW" and downgrades for imprecision because "at k = 3 tau-squared is estimated from almost no information and a random-effects interval is optimistic even when it looks tight."
Uncertainty is represented with brutal honesty. It refuses to draw underpowered plots ("A funnel plot and its asymmetry tests have almost no power below about ten trials... It is declined rather than drawn") and refuses to fake a protocol ("A protocol specified before data collection is a historical fact about the past and cannot be created retrospectively.").

**WHICH IS BETTER?**

Document B is decisively better. 

What separates them is that Document B interrogates the actual scientific constructs being combined, whereas Document A blindly feeds numbers into an undisclosed model. Document B treats trial endpoints as specific, registered definitions that cannot be mixed if they differ, and treats its own statistical tools with appropriate skepticism (recognizing that a small-k random-effects interval is "optimistic even when it looks tight"). Document A completely ignores the fundamental requirement of meta-analysis—that you must prove you are combining apples with apples—and reports the output of an unstated model as absolute truth while hiding all heterogeneity, endpoint harmonization, and methodological uncertainty from the reader.
