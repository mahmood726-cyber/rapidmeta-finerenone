**Document A: DESK-REJECT.** Reason: **unsound method and unreadability**.

The question is clear enough, but the review disqualifies itself. It admits: “**No fresh systematic database search was run for this object**” and “**No second independent screen was run for this topic**.” That is not a minor reporting gap for a clinical systematic review. It also says the criteria were “**derived post hoc from the trials**,” then uses those criteria to decide what can be pooled.

There are internal contradictions. It says “**Risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2**,” but the certainty table says “**No result-level RoB 2 assessment exists for this outcome**.” It says the four-trial pool “**mixed the two definitions**” and “**remains withdrawn**,” yet still shows “**Figure 1. Forest plot -- cardiovascular death or a worsening heart failure event... k = 4**.” That is not review-ready.

To move to review: run and report a real reproducible systematic search, add independent duplicate screening/extraction or justify the absence, remove the withdrawn four-trial forest plot, reconcile the risk-of-bias contradictions, provide a normal manuscript structure, and make the GRADE/RoB judgments auditable without self-referential clutter.

**Document B: SEND FOR PEER REVIEW.** Reason: the question and method are clear enough for external review.

It states a defined population, intervention, comparator, outcomes, sources, and dates: “**randomised, placebo-controlled, cardiovascular outcome trials of SGLT2i in patients with type 2 diabetes**” and “**We searched PubMed and Embase for trials published up to Sept 24, 2018**.” It gives usable pooled estimates, event counts, subgroup analyses, and clinical interpretation.

Concerns for reviewers: only three trials, search limited to PubMed and Embase, duplicate extraction is not fully clear because “**discrepancies were resolved by consensus**” does not prove independent extraction, and risk-of-bias methods are absent from this excerpt. But those are peer-review issues, not desk-reject issues.

**Better: B, decisively better.** B is readable, conventionally structured, and methodologically reviewable. A is more transparent about its defects, but those defects are fatal: no fresh systematic search, no duplicate screening, contradictions in risk of bias, and a withdrawn analysis still displayed as a figure.
