Estimate withdrawn. The numerators and the denominators on this page come from different outcomes, on all three trials. Each row carried the primary composite's participant denominators -- 3,889 and 3,865; 2,654 and 2,641; 5,470 and 5,469, all exactly right -- against event counts of 8 and 5, 6 and 18, and 18 and 18. Those counts are not the composite's. They are all-cause mortality, a secondary outcome on every one of the three: CHAMPION-PCI's 'Individual Incidence of All-cause Mortality' is 8 against 5, CHAMPION-PLATFORM's 'Incidence of All-cause Mortality' is 6 against 18, and CHAMPION-PHOENIX's 18 against 18 is the death row of its stent-thrombosis/death/MI breakdown. The registry's own primary-composite counts are 290 and 276, 185 and 210, and 257 and 322 -- between one and two orders of magnitude larger than what the page used. A mortality numerator over a composite denominator is not a small error in a rate; it is a quantity that corresponds to nothing. And the correction reverses the conclusion, which is why this matters more than a tidiness complaint. Pooling the registry's own primary counts by DerSimonian-Laird gives OR 0.8955 (0.7526 to 1.0656), I-squared 64 per cent -- an interval that crosses no effect. The card published OR 0.81 (0.71 to 0.91), which excludes it. The page reported a significant benefit where the trials' own primary outcomes do not establish one. Separately and sufficient on its own: the composites are not the same. CHAMPION-PHOENIX's registered primary is 'The Composite Incidence of All-cause Mortality, Myocardial Infarction (MI), Ischemia-driven Revascularization (IDR) and stent thrombosis (ST)' -- four components. CHAMPION-PCI's and CHAMPION-PLATFORM's are mortality, MI and IDR: three. One whole component is present in one trial and absent from the other two, so even with correct counts these three would not share one estimand.

What is confirmed, stated as prominently as what is wrong. (1) the trials are the right trials and the identities are correct: NCT00305162, NCT00385138 and NCT01156571 are champion-pci, champion-platform and CHAMPION-PHOENIX. (2) every arm size is exactly right. All six denominators match the registry's primary-outcome record to the participant. That is what makes the defect so hard to see: five of the six numbers in each 2x2 are correct. (3) the arm roles and labels are correct on all three -- cangrelor against clopidogrel, matching each registration's own arm titles. What this withdrawal does not establish: not that cangrelor is ineffective. CHAMPION-PHOENIX's own primary result, 257 of 5,470 against 322 of 5,469, is OR 0.7880 (0.6659 to 0.9326) and favours cangrelor. CHAMPION-PLATFORM's is 0.8674 (0.7063 to 1.0652) and CHAMPION-PCI's is 1.0478 (0.8830 to 1.2434). All three are on this page. Results

Death, myocardial infarction or ischaemia-driven revascularisation at 48 hours -- and stent thrombosis in one of the three trials. These 3 trials are not pooled, and the reason is stated rather than the outcome being quietly omitted: The numerators and the denominators on this page come from different outcomes, on all three trials. Each row carried the primary composite's participant denominators -- 3,889 and 3,865; 2,654 and 2,641; 5,470 and 5,469, all exactly right -- against event counts of 8 and 5, 6 and 18, and 18 and 18. Those counts are not the composite's. They are all-cause mortality, a secondary outcome on every one of the three: CHAMPION-PCI's 'Individual Incidence of All-cause Mortality' is 8 against 5, CHAMPION-PLATFORM's 'Incidence of All-cause Mortality' is 6 against 18, and CHAMPION-PHOENIX's 18 against 18 is the death row of its stent-thrombosis/death/MI breakdown. The registry's own primary-composite counts are 290 and 276, 185 and 210, and 257 and 322 -- between one and two orders of magnitude larger than what the page used. A mortality numerator over a composite denominator is not a small error in a rate; it is a quantity that corresponds to nothing. And the correction reverses the conclusion, which is why this matters more than a tidiness complaint. Pooling the registry's own primary counts by DerSimonian-Laird gives OR 0.8955 (0.7526 to 1.0656), I-squared 64 per cent -- an interval that crosses no effect. The card published OR 0.81 (0.71 to 0.91), which excludes it. The page reported a significant benefit where the trials' own primary outcomes do not establish one. Separately and sufficient on its own: the composites are not the same. CHAMPION-PHOENIX's registered primary is 'The Composite Incidence of All-cause Mortality, Myocardial Infarction (MI), Ischemia-driven Revascularization (IDR) and stent thrombosis (ST)' -- four components. CHAMPION-PCI's and CHAMPION-PLATFORM's are mortality, MI and IDR: three. One whole component is present in one trial and absent from the other two, so even with correct counts these three would not share one estimand.1

Noted on this pool. The 0.81 this page records as the withdrawn card's figure is also the published one. Steg et al., Lancet 2013 (PMID 24011551) -- a prespecified pooled analysis of PATIENT-LEVEL data from all three CHAMPION trials, 24,910 patients -- reports the primary composite at OR 0.81 (0.71 to 0.91). The withdrawal note on this outcome records that the card published OR 0.81 (0.71 to 0.91). The same value and the same interval. It does not undo the withdrawal. This object establishes that the card's own stored numerators were all-cause mortality counts set against the primary composite's denominators, on all three trials, and that is a real data defect. What it changes is the reading: the card's headline value appears to have been right while the counts beneath it could not produce it. A correct number standing on the wrong arithmetic is a provenance failure, not an accuracy one, and the withdrawal note currently reads as though the benefit itself were unsupported. From published aggregates this review can pool only CHAMPION-PCI and CHAMPION-PLATFORM on the three-component composite, because CHAMPION phoenix (NCT01156571) registers a four-component composite adding stent thrombosis -- its counts are recorded here, 257/5470 against 322/5469, so the exclusion is checkable. Steg et al. had patient-level data and could rebuild the three-component quantity for phoenix; that is not recoverable from aggregates. The two-trial pool is 0.965 (0.813 to 1.14) and crosses no difference where theirs does not. Prefer the published analysis. Three trials at patient level, prespecified, beats two trials of aggregate counts. Unresolved, and not decided here: whether this outcome's withdrawal note should be restated now that the withdrawn figure is corroborated by the literature. That is a content decision about a published number and belongs to the author. About the corrected three-component composite pool on this object, recorded here because that is where a reader arrives. The stored pooled result on this object names no effect measure. The `pooled` block holds point, ci_low, ci_high and ci_level and NO `measure` field: {"point": 0.9646, "ci_low": 0.8132, "ci_high": 1.1442, "ci_level": 95}. The outcome block above it says RR, so a reader who looks in the right place can recover it -- but the number itself travels without its unit, and it is compared elsewhere on this object against a published odds ratio. A risk ratio and an odds ratio are not interchangeable: away from the null the odds ratio is further from 1 than the risk ratio, so comparing ours against theirs makes our result look closer to no effect than a like-for-like comparison would. The direction of that error is knowable and is stated here; the size is not, because it depends on the event rate they pooled over, which this object does not hold. About the corrected three-component composite pool on this object, recorded here because that is where a reader arrives. Where this pool and the published one differ, the cause named on this object is not the one the evidence points to. The cangrelor PCI programme's central controversy is the definition of the myocardial infarction component of its composite primary, which changed during the programme -- the third universal definition of myocardial infarction was published in 2012, mid-programme -- and the trials' results are sensitive to which definition is applied. A composite whose largest component is redefined is not the same endpoint before and after, and two syntheses applying different definitions will differ for that reason before any statistical one. This is recorded as the likely cause and not as an established one: establishing it needs the definition each synthesis applied, which is not on this object.2

The full model output for every pooled outcome -- the call, the estimator, the heterogeneity statistics and the back-transformed interval -- is reproduced verbatim as Extended data at the end of this article, so every number above can be checked against the software that produced it.3
