Pooled result

Estimate withdrawn. The published estimate is the comparator, not the finding. The index card served 'Published: HR 0.81 (0.73 to 0.91), k=4'. This page's own bytes embed PUBLISHED_META_BENCHMARKS = Ruff 2014, 'Ruff pooled doac vs warfarin MA (stroke/SE)', measure RR, estimate .81, lci .73, uci .91, k 4, Lancet 2014;383:955-962 -- a locked local database of published syntheses this review is meant to be compared against. All four numbers on the card are those four numbers. This object's own pooled value is OR 0.7817 (0.6710 to 0.9108) at k=3 and shares no digit with the card. The one place the page held a correct external value is the value that migrated into the claim position, and the measure was relabelled from RR to HR on the way. Separately and sufficient on its own: k is overstated. The card and the page's AUTO_INCLUDE_TRIAL_IDS both say four trials; three are pooled. Rocket af carries no event counts on the page, so a pool derived from 2x2 counts drops it in silence, and it is the trial whose registered result is closest to no effect. Separately again: the measure is misnamed. Every per-trial value here is an odds ratio this project derived from 2x2 counts -- the object says so in its own derivation_note -- while all four registry records register a Cox proportional-hazards analysis of time to first event and report a hazard ratio. A derived odds ratio was published under the label HR.

What is confirmed, stated as prominently as what is wrong. (1) the four endpoint definitions agree in their components. All four registry records count the first occurrence of stroke or systemic embolism: RE-LY 'stroke or systemic embolic event', rocket af 'a stroke or non-CNS systemic embolism', aristotle 'Ischemic/Unspecified Stroke, Hemorrhagic Stroke, or Systemic Embolism', engage 'the composite of stroke and Systemic Embolic Events'. That is real agreement, read from four independent records, and it is not the reason this estimate is withdrawn. (2) aristotle's per-trial value on the page is exactly right. The page publishes 0.79 (0.66 to 0.95) and the registry's own Cox analysis is 0.79 (.66 to .95), digit for digit. (3) aristotle's and engage's stored counts reconcile exactly to the registry -- 212 = 159 + 38 + 15 and 265 = 173 + 76 + 16 for aristotle; 296 of 7035 and 337 of 7036 are engage's registered superiority row. (4) every comparison points the right way. On all three pooled trials the arm carrying role treatment holds the doac's counts. The arm labels were wrong on all three and have been corrected here from the registry's arm sizes; not one role moved. What this withdrawal does not establish, written here so it is not read for more than it says: Not that these four trials cannot be pooled. Their components agree, and the published literature pools exactly these four for exactly this composite. What cannot stand is this pool: three count-derived odds ratios where four registered hazard ratios exist, a fourth trial dropped in silence, and a headline that is the comparator. The replacement question is identified and deliberately not taken. It is to pool the four registered hazard ratios rather than derive odds ratios from counts. It is not taken because reading the registry to establish the above meant reading those hazard ratios, so their answer is already known to whoever would choose the question -- the same rule that parked ABLATION_AF and SGLT2_CKD. It also has a real obstacle that has to be decided by a person and not guessed: Engage registers five primary analyses of one composite whose hazard ratios run 0.79, 0.86 and 0.87, and rocket af's registered analyses are both 'while on treatment' while aristotle's is an intended-treatment period. Choosing among those is choosing the estimand. Parked.
