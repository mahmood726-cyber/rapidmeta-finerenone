# The corpus split — and the headline was measuring the wrong thing

Reading the 33 unclassified objects was meant to close a gap in a denominator. It found
something larger: **19 topics carry a live pooled estimate that this programme has never
audited.**

## The 33, read

| | n |
|---|---|
| **carrying a live pooled estimate, never audited here** | **19** |
| closed with a withdrawal already recorded | 8 |
| neither — closed without a withdrawal flag, or k=1 | 6 |

They were unclassified for a mundane reason: they **predate the verdict schema**. No
`topic_state`, no `which_limb_fails`. Not damaged — just older than the fields the
classifier reads.

## What that does to the headline

**"Nine of 53 cardiology topics publish a pool" and "13 of 62 in infectious disease" both
counted only topics this programme opened.** They are accurate as stated and they were
never the corpus-wide number. These 19 publish pooled estimates too — they were simply
never in the audit's field of view.

**So the honest corpus statement has three categories, not two:**

1. **Built and audited here** — every input traced to a registration, provenance stated,
   what-it-does-not-establish attached.
2. **Carrying an estimate, not audited** — 19 topics. **The estimate is live on the page
   and no one has checked it against the registrations.**
3. **Closed with a sourced verdict** — the 55 impossible, plus 8 of these 33.

**"Roughly one topic in five supports a pool" was never wrong, but it answers a narrower
question than it appears to** — how many of the topics we opened could be built. It says
nothing about topics we never opened, and 19 of those are publishing numbers.

## The one that matters most

**`arni-hfref` is in the unaudited 19.** k=4, pooled point **0.8715**, live.

That is the flagship topic — the one where the ANSWER-HF finding was established, and the
most important methodological result this programme has produced. **The finding was
established against the registrations; the pooled estimate sitting on the object was not
re-derived afterwards.** Those are different acts and only the first was done.

**This is not a claim that 0.8715 is wrong.** It is a statement that nothing in the record
says it is right, on the topic where we have the most reason to look.

## The pending question, answered — and it goes the other way

**`PENDING` is one topic, not 22.** Twenty-one of the 22 are **k=1**: the page seeds a
single trial. That is a **topic-list defect**, not a data gap — it becomes buildable only
if someone adds trials to the page, which is a scope decision, not an event that happens
on its own.

**Only `MALARIA_ACT` is genuinely waiting on data**: its limbs all pass and none of its
five registrations posts results.

**This strengthens the headline rather than weakening it.** The concern was that a
material share of the un-pooled topics were merely waiting and would resolve themselves.
They are not. **The un-pooled topics are un-poolable, and the one exception is named.**

## What this does not establish

**NOT that the 19 are wrong.** None has been checked. Several may be sound; the
`finerenone` and `sglt2` topics in particular come from the corpus's original and most
worked-on area. **NOT that they are comparable to the audited builds** — they carry no
provenance statement, no per-trial source URLs read on a stated date, and no
what-this-does-not-establish block, so even a correct estimate is not checkable in the way
the audited ones are.

**NOT a new count of how many corpus topics support a pool.** That number needs the 19
audited first, and this file deliberately does not produce one. **The gap is now named and
measured; it is not closed.**
