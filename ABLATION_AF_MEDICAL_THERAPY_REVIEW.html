Methods — synthesis

Risk of bias was assessed with RoB 2 (Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials) (22 August 2019 version, as reproduced in the Cochrane Handbook), following Higgins JPT, Savovic J, Page MJ, Elbers RG, Sterne JAC. Chapter 8: Assessing risk of bias in a randomized trial. In: Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions version 6.5.1. A result, not a study -- Handbook 8.2: 'risk of bias is assessed for a specific result'. Every judgement below names its outcome. 3 result-level assessments were made. A domain that cannot be judged from the registration and the published report is no information, never LOW. Low-by-default asserts a fact; high-by-default invents a defect.1

A ceiling applies to every risk-of-bias judgement in this review; it is stated with the assessment itself rather than summarised here.2

Certainty of evidence was rated with GRADE, following Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions version 6.5.1, Chapter 14: Completing 'Summary of findings' tables and grading the certainty of the evidence. HIGH for a body of randomised evidence, then rated down with reasons. 0 pooled outcome(s) were rated. No domain is rated UP. Rating up for large effect or dose-response applies to observational evidence that has not already been rated down, and neither condition holds here. 1 outcome(s) were not rated because their pool is declined or withdrawn: there is no effect estimate to rate the certainty of, and rating one would be certainty about a number this review refused to publish.3

Results

Each trial's own registered primary composite. These 3 trials are not pooled, and the reason is stated rather than the outcome being quietly omitted: The three trials register three different primary composites and the registry states all three. CASTLE-AF counts all-cause mortality or worsening heart failure requiring unplanned hospitalisation. Cabana counts total mortality, disabling stroke, serious bleeding or cardiac arrest. RAFT-AF counts all-cause mortality and heart-failure events, where an event includes an outpatient intravenous diuretic visit. No two of the three count the same events: one adds stroke and bleeding, one adds outpatient heart-failure care, one counts neither. An average over them is an average over three questions.1
