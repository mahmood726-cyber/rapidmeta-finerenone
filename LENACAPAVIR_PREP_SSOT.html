Cochrane Handbook sections: 10.3.3, 10.8, 10.4.4, 10.10.1, 10.10.3

ONE reason is sufficient and it is arithmetic rather than judgement: PURPOSE 1's rate ratio is exactly zero, so it has no logarithm, and the generic inverse-variance method that sections 10.3.3 and 10.8 prescribe for combining rate ratios operates on log ratios. There is nothing to give it. Section 10.4.4 is cited for what it actually covers -- rare events in two-by-two data, where it recommends the Peto method and warns that adding a half to each cell 'usually has the undesirable effect of biasing study estimates towards no difference'. That warning is about CELLS and these are RATES, so it is cited as the nearest guidance rather than as governing; a reviewer objected to an earlier version that cited it as though it governed, and was right. A Peto odds-ratio synthesis on the participant counts IS computable and is not performed for two stated reasons: it would silently change the estimand from the rate ratio both trials published to an odds ratio on participants, and both trials allocated unequally, which is the condition 10.4.4 identifies as biasing that method. The population difference between the trials is recorded as clinical diversity under 10.10.1 and is NOT claimed to be independently sufficient -- an earlier version did claim that, which is stricter than the Handbook, since 10.10.3 treats declining to synthesise as a judgement about whether an average would mislead and random-effects models exist precisely to carry such diversity.

The comparator is an incidence estimated in the screened population, not a randomised group. It carries the assumptions of that estimate and is never combined with the randomised contrast.

The methods rule governing this decision

Decision: REPORT each trial separately; compute NO pooled estimate, and never combine this with the randomised contrast

Cochrane Handbook sections: 3.2.3, 10.3.3, 10.8, 10.10.1

The comparator is not a randomised group. Section 3.2.3 distinguishes the kinds of comparison a review may make, and an estimate against an incidence modelled in a screened population is not the same quantity as a contrast between two randomised arms: it carries the assumptions of that model and none of the protection randomisation provides. The two are reported as separate outcomes for that reason. No synthesis across trials is computed here either, and the governing reason is the same arithmetic one as for the randomised contrast -- one trial's rate ratio is zero and the generic inverse-variance method of 10.3.3 and 10.8 has no log ratio to take. An earlier version invoked that reason here while citing only the comparison-definition and heterogeneity sections, which did not govern it.
