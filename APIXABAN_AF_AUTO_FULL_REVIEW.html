Pooled result

Estimate withdrawn. Two of these four trials register an efficacy endpoint and two register bleeding, and the two point in opposite directions. Verbatim from the registry: ARISTOTLE's primary is 'Number of Participants With First Event of Ischemic/Unspecified Stroke, Hemorrhagic Stroke, or Systemic Embolism (SE) During the Intended Treatment Period'; AVERROES's is 'Event Rate of Stroke/Systemic Embolism During the Intended-treatment Period'. PACIFIC-AF's is 'Number of Participants With Composite of International Society on Thrombosis and Hemostasis (isth) Major Bleeding or Clinically Relevant Non-major (CRNM) Bleeding'; RENAL-AF's is 'Number of Participants Experiencing isth Major or Clinically Relevant Non-major Bleeding'. A stroke prevented and a bleed caused are not the same event, and for an anticoagulant they move in opposite directions -- so averaging them does not produce a weaker estimate of either, it produces a number that estimates neither and whose direction depends on the mix of trials. Separately: AVERROES registers an event rate rather than a count of participants, so a count-derived odds ratio is not its registered estimand even before the efficacy-safety mismatch is reached. Separately again: the two bleeding trials are small -- PACIFIC-AF 249 against 254, RENAL-AF 82 against 72 -- beside ARISTOTLE's 9,120 against 9,081, so the pooled direction was always going to be set by the efficacy trials while the estimand was contaminated by the safety ones.

What is confirmed, stated as prominently as what is wrong. (1) the original withdrawal was right, and it is confirmed here against the registry rather than accepted on trust: the card's one-line reason, 'bleeding and efficacy endpoints pooled', is exactly what the four registered primaries say. This re-verification could have reversed it and did not. (2) aristotle's row is exactly correct: 212 of 9,120 against 265 of 9,081 are the registry's own primary-outcome counts, and 212 = 159 + 38 + 15 and 265 = 173 + 76 + 16 across its three reported categories. (3) the four trial identities are correct. What this withdrawal does not establish: not that apixaban does not prevent stroke. ARISTOTLE and AVERROES both favour it on their own registered efficacy endpoints, and those two are the trials that were powered for it. What cannot stand is a single pooled number built from two efficacy trials and two bleeding trials. The replacement question is identified and deliberately not taken: ARISTOTLE and AVERROES share an efficacy estimand and could be synthesised, but their comparators differ -- warfarin in one, aspirin in the other -- and choosing whether that is one question is an estimand decision. Parked.
