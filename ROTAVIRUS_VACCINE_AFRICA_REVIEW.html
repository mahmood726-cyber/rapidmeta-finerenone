WITHDRAWN — 2026-09-10

Rotavirus vaccines versus placebo, severe rotavirus gastroenteritis in Africa: this review's pooled estimate is withdrawn

This page previously served a pooled efficacy estimate for this question. That estimate has been withdrawn. The original page is preserved byte for byte and is linked below; nothing has been deleted. This notice is deliberately better sourced than the page it replaces — every trial below carries a resolvable registration and a PMID.

No pooled estimate is published for this question. The withdrawn pool published OR 0.4941 and a derived "~51% efficacy"; an unpublished re-derivation is shown near the end, explicitly not published and not promoted. No published pooled estimate stands in the withdrawn one's place.

What is withdrawn

The pooled estimate OR 0.4941 (95% CI 0.3466 to 0.7045) across three trials, and the derived headline "roughly 51% efficacy" (computed as 1 − 0.4941).

Why — vaccine efficacy was computed as 1 − odds ratio

Vaccine efficacy is 1 − RR, 1 − IRR, or 1 − HR. It is never 1 − OR. This page pooled odds ratios and subtracted the pooled odds ratio from 1 to obtain an efficacy percentage. That is a category error that biases the efficacy figure.

The registry record we read states the measure in its own field, verbatim:

paramType: "Efficacy = 1-Relative Risk"

We read that field, discarded the stated measure, and computed an odds ratio instead. Two of the three trials computed efficacy from person-time incidence rates (Armah: 79/2610.6 person-years vs 129/2585.9; Isanaka: 2.14 vs 6.44 per 100 person-years) — person-time denominators this page discarded entirely.

All three 2×2 tables are wrong; one is unsourceable

Every contributing 2×2 table mixed a per-protocol numerator with a randomised denominator. Worse, the Rotarix cells are fabricated:

Rotarix, as served: 21 / 1042 vs 37 / 1047 1042 + 1047 = 2089 = the study-level enrollmentInfo.count, SPLIT IN TWO. The true randomised arm sizes (1647 / 1651 / 1641) sit in the participant-flow module of the same registry record, and were not used.

A study-level integer was taken and two arm denominators were invented from it. The object underlying this page records the mismatch of its own accord — two trials carry a provenance_MISMATCH field stating the counts do not match any posted outcome, and Rotasiil (NCT02145000) posts no results section at all, so its counts cannot have come from the registry this page's masthead claims as its source.

The consequence, named

Our served Rotarix efficacy read 43.9%. The published and registry-posted figure is 61.2% (44.0 to 73.2). The extraction error moved the pool toward the null, understated heterogeneity (true I² ≈ 71%, served 57.6%), and manufactured the caveat the page was built around.

The headline caveat was an artefact, and our explanation of it was false on our own numbers

The page said its Hartung–Knapp interval crossed no-effect because of disagreement between the trials. That is false on the page's own arithmetic: the variance-inflation factor is q = 0.920 < 1, so the floor bound applied and the HK standard error equalled the unadjusted one — the entire widening was t(2) = 4.3027 versus z = 1.96, i.e. small-k, not between-trial dispersion. There is less dispersion than the model expects, the opposite of what the page claimed. With the Rotarix cells corrected the crossing disappears: OR 0.438 (0.308–0.623), Hartung–Knapp 0.202–0.949, which excludes no-effect.

Harms

The page reported no harms at all, including intussusception — the signature safety concern for oral rotavirus vaccines. For a vaccine review that is disqualifying on its own.

What is currently known — NOT PUBLISHED AND NOT PROMOTED

Stated only so this withdrawal is not read as denying the vaccines work. The three trials' own reported efficacies, each from its publication and registration:

trialregistrationPMIDreported VE
Rotarix, South Africa & Malawi (Madhi) NCT00241644 2010721461.2%
RotaTeq, Ghana/Kenya/Mali (Armah) NCT00362648 2069203039.3%
Rotasiil, Niger (Isanaka) NCT02145000 2832834666.7%

A pool of the three, computed correctly, is approximately VE 56% (37 to 70%), with I² ≈ 71%, Q 7.09, p = 0.029.

The reviewer's judgement is that these should probably not be pooled at all: three different vaccines, three different settings, and follow-up windows differing roughly twofold and never disclosed on the page (which is exactly why two of the trials used person-time). This re-derivation has not cleared review; it is shown for transparency, not as a replacement claim.

A trial never found, and an arm silently dropped

  • An eligible trial was never retrieved: NCT04010448, 4,055 African infants. The page's search retrieved 50 of 1,452 PubMed records by registry reconciliation rather than screening, and its eligibility criteria are recorded as "not recorded", so no exclusion of this trial can even be checked.
  • An entire randomised arm was dropped: the Rotarix two-dose arm (n = 1647), present in the trial's participant-flow, does not appear in the pool.
