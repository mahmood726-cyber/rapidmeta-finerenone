# Full-text requests, ranked

**Four items, one dropped.** Each names the document, the topic, the exact figure or
statement to read, and what happens if we don't get it. **Two of the candidates turned out
to be the same document**, which is why the list is shorter than the shortlist.

---

## 1. FDA or EMA review of the gepotidacin programme (EAGLE-2 / EAGLE-3)

**Topic:** `gepotidacin-urinary-tract` — uncomplicated urinary tract infection.
**Trials:** NCT04020341 (EAGLE-2), NCT04187144 (EAGLE-3).

**What to read, in priority order:**
1. **Pathogen and resistance profile at enrolment, by trial** — specifically the
   nitrofurantoin susceptibility distribution and the *E. coli* share in each.
2. **Geographic or site distribution by trial.**
3. **Any statement about between-trial differences in how therapeutic response was
   adjudicated in practice** (as opposed to how it was defined — the definitions are
   already known to be identical).
4. **Any pooled estimate the reviewer computes across the two trials**, with its estimator.

**What it unblocks — and this is why it is first.** Two separate blockers, one document.
The topic publishes **RR 1.2007 (0.9668–1.4912) with I²=70.5%** and the object currently
says *"this topic has a pooled number and not a conclusion."* Registered eligibility and
endpoint definitions are **identical** across the two trials — already checked, the cheap
explanation ruled out — so **the registry has nothing left to give.** Item 4 also makes this
the published-synthesis comparator the object records as owed.

**If we don't get it:** the topic stays published with the heterogeneity warning and no
explanation. That is honest but it is a live estimate we cannot interpret, and it is the
only one of our built pools in that state.

## 2. FDA or EMA guidance on complicated intra-abdominal infection trial design, **or** the eravacycline regulatory review

**Topic:** `eravacycline-infection`. **Trials:** NCT01844856 (vs ertapenem), NCT02784704
(vs meropenem).

**What to read:** whether **ertapenem and meropenem are treated as one acceptable active
comparator class** for cIAI — either in trial-design guidance, or by a reviewer pooling the
two trials despite the different comparators.

**What it unblocks:** the corpus's **only REFERRED topic.** It currently displays no pool
pending a scope decision that is Mahmood's. A regulatory document converts that from a
judgement into a sourced decision — the same discipline as citing the Handbook rather than
reasoning from first principles. **The counts are already recorded** (191/220 vs 198/226;
177/195 vs 187/205), so if the answer is "one class", the pool is built in minutes.

**If we don't get it:** the decision still gets made, but on clinical plausibility rather
than on a source. Note the two are **not** interchangeable in general — ertapenem lacks
reliable antipseudomonal activity — so the question is narrowly whether that matters *in a
control arm* for this infection.

## 3. Kosiborod et al., *NEJM* 2024;390:1394–1407 (STEP-HFpEF DM) — **results table**

**Topic:** `incretin-hfpef-review`. **What to read:** the **KCCQ clinical summary score
treatment difference from the results table**, with its CI — to compare against the
abstract's *"estimated difference, 7.3 points; 95% CI, 4.1 to 10.4."* Also **the NCT number
printed on the paper.**

**What it unblocks:** the **only mixed-provenance pool in the corpus.** Every other number
we publish is registry-sourced; this one input is a publication abstract, and the object
says explicitly that a results table should supersede it. The *Lancet* pooled analysis was
checked and **carries no KCCQ result**, so that route is closed.

**Honest about the likely outcome:** the table value will probably equal the abstract
value. **What changes is the provenance tier, not the number** — and the identity check,
which is currently one-directional. Worth one retrieval for a structural property of the
corpus; not worth two.

## 4. FDA or EMA review of the lefamulin programme (LEAP 1 / LEAP 2)

**Topic:** `lefamulin-cabp` / `lefamulin-cap`. **What to read:** any pooled estimate across
the two trials on early clinical response, with its estimator.

**What it unblocks:** the published-comparator gap only. **This is the weakest of the four
and should be dropped if retrievals are scarce.** The pool is clean — **RR 0.9884
(0.9530–1.0250), I²=0%, τ²=0**, both trials the same drug against the same comparator on
the same endpoint. **Nothing about it is unresolved.** A regulatory pool would confirm an
uncontested number.
