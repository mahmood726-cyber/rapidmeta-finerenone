The event counted is CURE, the beneficial category. That choice is declared because the section governing the measure asks for it explicitly, and because the complementary choice gives a different answer, which is reported in the sensitivity block rather than hidden.

The methods rule governing this decision

Decision: POOL the three trials that share indication, comparator, outcome and analysis population, on the risk ratio of cure, by random effects; and report in the same breath that the exclusion of the null does not survive the alternatives the Handbook asks to be tested

Cochrane Handbook sections: 3.2.4, 10.4.3, 10.10.1, 10.10.2, 10.10.4, 10.14

Section 10.4.3 recommends that meta-analyses be undertaken using risk ratios or odds ratios and asks for a sensible, declared choice of which category is the event; the risk ratio of cure is used and the event category is declared on the outcome. That section also warns that selecting an effect measure BECAUSE it is the most consistent is not a recommended strategy, since it can spuriously maximise precision, and this object is bound by that warning: the risk ratio is not chosen for its lower inconsistency, it is chosen as one of the two measures the section recommends and on its ease of interpretation, which the same section discusses. The odds ratio is reported beside it. What the pooled trials differ in is recorded under 10.10.1 rather than treated as disqualifying, and the inconsistency statistic is reported in the bands of 10.10.2. Section 10.14, with 10.4.3's own instruction to test the choice of statistic and of event category, is why the sensitivity block exists and why its result is reported as prominently as the primary line. Section 10.10.4 is what governs the MODEL and the estimator, which the effect-measure sections do not: it records that estimators other than DerSimonian and Laird perform better and that restricted maximum likelihood is now the default in the Cochrane software, and its subsection on few studies advises comparing the methods when there are only two or three. That comparison is in the sensitivity block, on all three estimators and on both interval methods.

Is the finding robust to how it was reached?

Section 10.4.3 says in terms that it may be wise to test whether the choice of summary statistic AND of which category counts as the event is critical to the conclusions, and section 10.14 is the general authority for testing arbitrary or unclear decisions. Here the answer is that it IS critical, and that is the most important thing this object has to report.

The three variance estimators agree closely, which is what the Handbook reports is usual: they can differ substantially in the heterogeneity they estimate but seldom move the summary effect much. The interval method is what matters here. Under the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman interval the null is no longer excluded, and section 10.10.4.5 says exactly what to make of that: with three studies that interval is likely to be too wide, while the ordinary one is likely to be too narrow, so neither is taken as decisive and both are shown.

The headline estimator is restricted maximum likelihood, which is what section 10.10.4.4 records as the default in the Cochrane software and the direction its evidence points: it says in terms that other methods for estimating the between-study variance perform better than DerSimonian and Laird. An earlier version of this object kept DerSimonian and Laird as the headline and gave as its reason that the local validator could recompute it. A reviewer was right that this is not a methodological reason at all, and that it mattered here, because the headline is the line that excludes the null. The tooling was the thing that had to move, not the method: the validator now recomputes restricted maximum likelihood independently, so the Handbook's default and external checkability are no longer in tension. DerSimonian and Laird is still shown beside it, and it makes almost no difference to the summary, which is what the Handbook says to expect.

Reconciliation against the published synthesis of the same literature

Carbapenems vs tigecycline for the treatment of complicated intra-abdominal infections: A Bayesian network meta-analysis. Medicine.

Both syntheses are OPEN ACCESS and both full texts were retrieved from PubMed Central and read, so the statements below are taken from methods sections and reference lists rather than from abstracts. An earlier version of this block said neither full text was retrievable. That was false, and it was false in the worst way available: no attempt had been made, while the PMC identifier for each sat in this object's own staged PubMed record. A reviewer read the staged record and found them. What remains genuinely unresolved is narrower and is stated below.

What matches

  • Whether the networks INCLUDED the trials pooled here
    For the carbapenem synthesis this is settled from its own text rather than inferred: it states that fifteen randomised trials were included and attaches the reference numbers of that set, and every trial pooled here carries a number inside it. So the syntheses and this object draw on the same trials for this comparison, and their far larger trial counts are scope rather than disagreement -- they answer which of many regimens is best, and this object answers one pairwise question. For the other synthesis all three appear in the reference list, which is evidence they were seen and not proof of inclusion, and it is left at that.
  • The direction of the comparison
    The network ranks clinical treatment success with imipenem/cilastatin ABOVE tigecycline. That is the direction this object finds directly, from the pairwise trials alone.
  • Which analysis population, and it settles a question raised in review
    The larger synthesis states its policy in its methods: it preferentially extracted a modified intention-to-treat population for clinical success where possible, and otherwise used the intention-to-treat population or the available data. That is exactly the BEST-AVAILABLE strategy this object runs as one of its two analysis-population sensitivity rows, and it was added only because a review leg argued that refusing it was stricter than the Handbook. A published synthesis in this indication doing the same thing is external support for that leg's objection, so it is recorded here rather than left as the object's own judgement call.
  • How strong the difference is
    The network reports the regimens as not statistically distinguishable. This object's pooled interval does exclude the null, but its own sensitivity block shows that exclusion failing under the odds ratio, under the complementary event, under the other analysis population and on two of three leave-one-out sets. A network on the odds ratio scale finding no distinguishable difference is what this object's own odds-ratio row finds too, so the two agree on the point that matters most.
  • The outcome
    The larger of the two syntheses names the clinical success rate as its primary outcome, which is the outcome this object pools.

What this object corrects

  • No estimate comparison is possible, and none is offered
    A network estimate on the odds ratio scale, pooled across many regimens, in which the figure for this pair mixes direct with indirect evidence.
    A DIRECT pairwise pooled risk ratio, from the trials that randomised this drug against this comparator and nothing else. These are different quantities on different scales, and setting them beside each other as though one checked the other would be the estimand error this batch exists to stop. Only the order and the strength are compared.

What could not be resolved

  • Exactly which arms the networks extracted per trial
    The larger synthesis names its extraction POLICY but not, in the text read here, the per-trial arm numbers it drew under it, and neither study-flow table was read to arm level. So no arm-by-arm comparison against this object's rows is offered.
  • What was withdrawn from this block, and why
    An earlier version said the included-study lists were not in the retrievable material and that whether the networks hold every trial pooled here could not be determined. Both were false once the open full texts were read, and both SURVIVED a round in which this object also carried the corrected finding beside them, so the block contradicted itself and two reviewers quoted the stale half back. The cause was a text substitution that missed its anchor and was not checked. Recorded rather than quietly deleted, because a reconciliation that silently drops what it got wrong is worth less than one that shows it.
